One day, weighed three ways

أَزْوَاجًا ثَلَاثَةً

Three lives

A day with every box ticked. The worst life a human being can live on this earth, written without softening. And the one in between, which the Qur'an says a great many people reach. The sorting is not ours; it is Al-Waqi'ah's.

أَزْوَاجًا ثَلَاثَةً

You become three kinds

The sorting in this room is not ours. The Qur'an does it, by name, in Al-Waqi'ah, and every section below is one of the three it names.

وَكُنتُمْ أَزْوَاجًا ثَلَاثَةً  ۝  فَأَصْحَابُ الْمَيْمَنَةِ مَا أَصْحَابُ الْمَيْمَنَةِ  ۝  وَأَصْحَابُ الْمَشْأَمَةِ مَا أَصْحَابُ الْمَشْأَمَةِ  ۝  وَالسَّابِقُونَ السَّابِقُونَ

“And you become of three kinds. The companions of the right, what are the companions of the right. And the companions of the left, what are the companions of the left. And the foremost, the foremost.”

Qur'an 56:7-10

Three lives follow. The first has every box ticked and is still in danger, and it is put first because it is the one people aim at. The second is the worst life a human being can live, written without softening, because a reader in it will recognise a flinch and close the page. The third is the one the Qur’an says a great many people reach, from the earliest generations and the later ones both, and it is the only one of the three built to be reached from where you are sitting.

What this room will not do

It will not tell you which of the three you are. Nobody on earth is in a position to tell you that, and the one place the sources are unanimous is that a man can look like the first and be the second, and look like the second and die as the first.

الْكِتَاب

One · The full ledger

A day with nothing left undone: every obligation performed, every voluntary act performed on top of it. Then the same day at the scale.

Read the two columns again

Notice what the ledger does not contain.

Every one of those twenty five entries is between a man and his Lord. Not one of them is between him and another human being, except the two that slipped in: his parents, and his earnings. That is the shape of the trap. A ledger built only of worship can be complete and still say nothing at all about how he treats a waiter, a wife, a tenant, or a man who cannot afford a lawyer.

This is not an argument against the twenty five. Keep them. It is an argument that they were never the whole accounting, and the sources say so in language far harder than ours.

Editorial our reading of what the ledger leaves out

The first way a full ledger fails

It can be paid out entirely to the people you wronged.

The hadith of the bankrupt one is not a warning about a bad man with no prayer. It is specifically about a man who arrives with prayer, fasting and zakat. His worship was real. It was also, at the scale, currency, and every person he insulted, cheated or struck was standing there with an invoice.

The practical consequence is unglamorous: a wrong done to a person is not settled on a prayer mat. It is settled with that person, while both of you are alive, in the form of the money returned, the apology made, the lie corrected in front of the people who heard it.

Sunnah Muslim 2581

The second way

The three most impressive entries are the three named in the hadith.

The Prophet ﷺ described the first three thrown into the Fire on the Day of Judgement, and they are not the ones anyone expects. A man killed in battle, who is told he fought so it would be said he was brave, and it was said. A man who learned and taught and recited the Qur’an, who is told he did it so it would be said he was learned, and it was said. A man who gave his wealth away, who is told he gave so it would be said he was generous, and it was said.

Read the ledger above one more time with that in mind. The three deeds most likely to be seen are the three in the hadith. This is why riya’, doing it to be seen, was called the hidden thing: nobody performs it on purpose, and nobody is safe from it.

Sunnah Muslim 1905

The third way

Some of it was never asked of you.

Three men came to the houses of the Prophet ﷺ asking about his worship. When they were told, they thought it too little for a man already forgiven. One said he would pray all night and never stop. One said he would fast every day and never break it. One said he would never marry.

He came to them and said: by Allah, I am the one among you with the most fear of Allah and the most awareness of Him, and yet I fast and I break my fast, I pray and I sleep, and I marry women. Whoever turns away from my way is not of me.

The maximal ledger is not the sunnah. The sunnah has sleep in it.

Sunnah Bukhari 5063
أَصْحَابُ الْمَشْأَمَة

Two · The worst life

Written plainly, because the person who needs this section will recognise softening immediately and stop reading.

The worst life a human being can live on this earth

He is sixty one. He owns four properties and cannot name a single person who would sit with him for an hour without wanting something. He has not spoken to his mother in nine years over a sum of money that would not now change his week. He tells the story of that argument well; he has had a long time to shape it.

His children learned from watching him that a person is a balance sheet, and they were quick learners, and now they treat him exactly as he taught them to. He finds this ungrateful. He has never once made the connection out loud.

He has taken from people who could not push back. A wage held for six weeks because the man had no papers and no lawyer. A partner cut out with a clause the partner did not read. A woman who was twenty three and believed him. He does not think of himself as having done any of this. He is not lying to you; he is a man who has simply never once run the sentence with himself as the subject.

There is no quiet in his day. Not one minute. The television is on when he eats. The phone is in his hand in the bathroom. He is not avoiding boredom, he is avoiding a question he decided at twenty two not to answer, and the machinery required to keep not answering it has been running for thirty nine years without a break.

And here is what makes it the worst life and not a cartoon: he is likeable. He is funny at dinner. He is generous when people are watching. Nobody at his funeral will say a bad word, and roughly nine people will come, and they will leave early, and the thing that will be missing from that room is not respectability. It is that not one person there will have been made better by having known him for sixty one years.

He was never poor. He was never in pain. Nothing bad ever happened to him. That is the point. This is the worst life available to a human being and it can be lived entirely in comfort, and it is far more common than the dramatic ruin people picture when they picture a life gone wrong.

Why this and not something more lurid

The Qur'an's own charge is the one above.

Scripture is available to be as graphic as it wishes, and about this it is not. It says they have hearts they do not understand with, eyes they do not see with, ears they do not hear with; that they are like cattle, rather more astray; and it names the condition: they are the heedless. The accusation is not appetite. It is the wasting of equipment that was issued for something.

And it names what such a life spent itself on: rivalry in increase distracted you, until you visited the graves. Not until you were confronted. Until you simply arrived, with the counting unfinished, the way it always is.

Qur'an 7:179Qur'an 102:1-2

The one thing that makes it irreversible

Not the sin. The refusal to look at it.

The heart in the figure below is not covered by a single catastrophe. It is covered a point at a time, and the hadith is precise about which point sticks: the one that is returned to. A sin repented of is polished off the heart entirely. A sin repeated without ever being named as a sin is the one that stays.

Which is why the man above is in more danger than a drunk who weeps about it. The drunk still has the word for what he is doing. The man above has replaced the word.

Scholars differ Tirmidhi 3334, graded hasan sahih by at-TirmidhiQur'an 83:14

The three the sources single out

Oppression, severed blood, and the orphan's money.

Sending Mu’adh to Yemen, the Prophet ﷺ told him to fear the supplication of the oppressed, for there is no veil between it and Allah. That is a strange sentence to sit with. It means the man who held that wage for six weeks has a petition filed against him that no procedure delays.

He also said the one who severs the ties of kinship does not enter Paradise. Nine years of not speaking to a mother is not a family disagreement in this frame; it is named.

And the Qur’an on those who consume the wealth of orphans unjustly: they are only consuming fire into their bellies, and they will burn in a blaze. There is no softer reading available. That is the verse.

Sunnah Bukhari 1496Sunnah Bukhari 5984Qur'an 4:10

And now the part that is not optional to include

A man came to a scholar having killed ninety nine people and asked whether repentance was possible. He was told no, and he killed him too, making a hundred. He asked again, of someone with more knowledge, and was told: who stands between you and repentance? He was told to leave his town, because the town itself was part of it, and to go to a place where Allah is worshipped. He died on the road. He was forgiven.

He was forgiven on the road, having completed nothing. That is the hadith, and it is in Bukhari and Muslim both, and it is there for exactly the reader who has got this far down the page and recognised himself.

“Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed Allah forgives all sins.” And the door stands open, the Prophet ﷺ said, until the death rattle reaches the throat. Not until you deserve it. Until you die.

Sunnah Bukhari 3470 · Muslim 2766Qur'an 39:53Scholars differ the death-rattle hadith, Tirmidhi 3537, graded hasan

Before you go on

If some of the above described you closely enough that it was unpleasant to read, that discomfort is not a verdict, and acting on it today matters far more than feeling it intensely. Pick the smallest reversible thing: the call to your mother, the money returned, the message sent to the person you cut. One. Today.

And if what is actually holding you is not a moral failure but something with a grip of its own, an addiction, a debt spiralling past what you can see, or a stretch where you have stopped wanting to be here, that is not a matter of praying harder. Speak to a doctor and to one human being you trust, this week. Seeking treatment is not a lapse in tawakkul. The Prophet ﷺ told his companions to seek treatment.

أَصْحَابُ الْيَمِين

Three · The balanced life

The Qur'an describes the companions of the right as a multitude from the earliest generations and a multitude from the later ones. It is a crowd. It was built to be reachable.

ثُلَّةٌ مِّنَ الْأَوَّلِينَ  ۝  وَثُلَّةٌ مِّنَ الْآخِرِينَ

“A multitude from the former peoples, and a multitude from the later peoples.”

Qur'an 56:39-40

Read that against the verses just above it, where the foremost are a multitude of the former peoples and a few of the later ones. The Qur’an is making a distinction and it is not hiding it: the very front rank thins out as the generations go on, and the right hand does not. Whatever else is true of the age you were born into, that door was not narrowed.

The floor, stated concretely

Not a mood. A list you could start tonight.

  • The five, on time. Not with perfect presence of heart. On time, wherever you happen to be standing. Presence comes later and it comes from this, not before it.
  • Something of the Qur'an daily, and the amount is genuinely allowed to be small. A page. Five verses with the meaning read alongside.
  • Your parents hear your voice. If they are gone, they hear your du'a.
  • Clean income. Everything else on this list is undermined if this one is rotten.
  • Your household experiences you as safe. Not as a project to be improved, not as an audience. Safe.
  • You give something small and regular, at an amount you will not stop.
  • You sleep, eat and move, because the body's right is one of the three and it is not the one you are allowed to spend.
  • You oppress nobody, and where you already have, you go to the person and not only to the prayer mat.
  • Istighfar, daily. He ﷺ sought forgiveness more than seventy times a day, and there was nothing on his record.
Sunnah Bukhari 1968Sunnah Bukhari 6307Editorial the arrangement into a floor is ours, not revelation

Why the small consistent thing is not a consolation prize

It is the ranked answer.

It would be easy to read the most beloved deeds are the most consistent, even if few as a kindness offered to people who cannot manage more. It is not phrased as a kindness. It is phrased as a ranking, and the small continuous thing is placed above the large discontinuous one.

He also said that this religion is ease, and that nobody makes it hard on himself without it defeating him. Not without it being difficult. Without it defeating him. That is a prediction about what happens to the man who tries to live the twenty five entry ledger on willpower in the third week.

Sunnah Bukhari 6464 · Muslim 783Sunnah Bukhari 39

The pass, and who is on the other side of it

The Qur'an defines it, then names them.

Al-Balad says a human being was shown the two ways and has not attempted the steep pass, then asks what could make you know what the steep pass is, and then, unusually, answers its own question. Freeing a neck. Feeding, on a day of hunger, an orphan who is a relative, or a poor person in the dust. And then being of those who believe, and who counsel one another to patience, and counsel one another to mercy.

And immediately after that definition: those are the companions of the right hand.

Look at what is in that list and what is not. Nothing in it requires a scholar, a title, a platform or an unusual capacity. Three of the four are things done to another human being who is worse off than you, and the fourth is refusing to let the people around you face difficulty alone. It is a definition you could satisfy this month.

Qur'an 90:10-18
What none of this promises

Not an easy life. Not a life without grief, illness, money trouble or the ordinary humiliations. The man who lived this pattern most completely buried six of his seven children. The promise attached to it is different and it is worth more: whoever does righteousness, male or female, while a believer, We will surely cause him to live a good life. A good one. Not a comfortable one.

الْمَوَازِين

Where the three arrive

Every road on this page ends at the same instrument, and the Qur'an describes it as a thing of exactness rather than a thing of threat.

Why the exactness is the mercy

Nothing is rounded off, in either direction.

A scale accurate to the weight of a mustard seed is frightening if you are only thinking about your sins. Turn it over. It also means the two minutes you spent listening to someone who had nobody else to tell, on a Tuesday nobody remembers, was weighed. The glass of water. The debt you quietly forgave. The temper you swallowed in a car park.

An imprecise scale would lose all of that in the noise. This one does not lose it. Whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it.

Qur'an 21:47Qur'an 99:7-8

The one line that decides which sentence you say

It is not a mystery, and it is not a lottery.

Al-Qari'ah puts it as plainly as it can be put: as for one whose scales are heavy, he is in a pleasant life; as for one whose scales are light, his refuge is an abyss. There is no third outcome offered and no appeal described.

What there is, and the whole of this page has been pointing at it, is the entire span of your life before that morning, in which the pans are still being loaded, by you, in quantities as small as a seed.

Qur'an 101:6-11
And the thing nobody on this page can tell you

Which pan your own life is currently loading. Not us, not a teacher, not a person who has watched you for thirty years. The sources are unanimous that a man can look like the first section and be the second, and look like the second and die as the first, and that certainty about your own standing is itself a warning sign rather than a good one. What is available to you is not the verdict. It is the next hour.

الْمِيزَان

Where you actually are

One question, asked honestly, is worth more than the whole page above.

Not which of the three am I. Nobody can answer that and the sources warn off anyone who tries. The useful question is smaller and it has an answer you already know:

If a person you have wronged and a person you have helped were both asked to describe you this evening, and neither knew you would hear it, which of the two would take longer to speak?

A question, not a ruling

If that question landed somewhere, it landed for a reason, and the thing to do with it is not to feel it more strongly. It is the one call, the one repayment, the one sentence of apology. Then Fajr tomorrow. Then the same again the day after, which is the only mechanism any of the three sections above actually run on.
