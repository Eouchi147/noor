A word of the Path

Makruh مَكْروه

Scholars differ

Disliked but not forbidden: leaving it is rewarded, doing it brings no sin.

What does Makruh mean in Islam?

Wasting water in wudu, and eating raw garlic before going to the mosque, are standard examples. Hanafis split the category in two: makruh tahriman, close to forbidden and sinful to do, and makruh tanzihan, merely unbecoming. The other three schools work with a single category. So a Hanafi and a Shafi'i can use the same word and mean noticeably different things, which is worth checking before you take offence or comfort.
