*Before anything else, a warning about what this is. I am not a scholar. I have no ijazah, no chain, no licence to rule on anything, and nothing on this page should be treated as an answer. What follows is a thought that has been turning in my head for years with nowhere to go, and I have written it down mainly so that someone who actually knows can tell me where it is wrong. If you are qualified, please correct me in the replies and bring the evidence with you. I would rather be corrected in public than comfortable in private.*

---

There is a suspicion that arrives late at night, usually in your twenties, usually when the house has gone quiet: the suspicion that this is not the real thing. It came to me at twenty four, in the same season that I began to pray, and it has never entirely left. Put plainly, the suspicion says that this life is a kind of staged room, that nothing inside it finally counts, and that whatever is real is waiting on the other side of the wall. Three words have to be untangled before the thought can even be examined: **dunya**, which the Arabic builds from a root meaning *nearer* and *lower*, the near life, the one within reach; **akhirah**, the last one, the one that comes after; and the modern word people now reach for, **simulation**, which carries a suggestion the other two never had, that what you are standing in is a fake. So the question I want to put to myself, and to anyone who will answer me, is this: **is this world false, or is it simply not final?** I want to take the suspicion seriously first, because I think a great deal of it is true and the Book says so more strongly than I ever did; then I want to show where it turns, in my own experience, from a clarity into a poison; and last I want to ask what is left of it on an ordinary Tuesday, when you have to get up and go to work.

## I. The suspicion, and how much of it is true

Consider a man who has lived his whole life in one room with a single window. The view through it is a valley, and it is beautiful, and it has never once occurred to him to touch the glass. Then one evening the light falls at a strange angle and he notices, for the first time, a seam in the sky. Nothing about his room has changed. Everything about his relationship to it has.

That is roughly what happened to me, and I assumed at the time that I had wandered somewhere unorthodox. I had not. It is stated at the centre of the Book, in language stronger than mine. *"This worldly life is nothing but diversion and play, and indeed the Home of the Hereafter, that is al-hayawan, the life, if only they knew"* (29:64). Read the word again, because it took me an embarrassingly long time to. It does not say the next life is better, or longer, or sweeter. **Al-hayawan** is the intensive: life itself, the thing the word was made to point at. The grammar quietly relocates reality to the other side of death and leaves this side holding the diversion.

And the scale is given in images, not arguments, which is why they survive being carried across fourteen centuries into a language the Prophet never heard. *"What is this world compared to the Hereafter except that one of you dips his finger in the sea, and let him see what it brings back"* (Muslim 2858). A film of water against every ocean there is. Then there is the answer given at the end, when the dead are asked how long they stayed on the earth, and the reply that comes back is not eighty years or a hundred, but *a day, or part of a day* (23:112). Whatever a life feels like from the inside, that is what it measures from outside.

Even the exit is rehearsed. Sleep and death are named with the same verb: *"Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that did not die, during their sleep"* (39:42). A Muslim performs a small version of the departure every night and calls it going to bed. So no, the suspicion is not exotic and it is not a defect of faith. It is closer to the plot than most of what I was taught in its place.

*Which is exactly why the next part matters so much, and why I have been afraid of it.*

## II. Where the suspicion turns into a poison

The seam in the sky is a true observation. What the man in the room does with it is another matter entirely. He might touch the glass and find it real, and understand that his room is small and temporary. Or he might decide that if there is a seam then nothing in the room was ever real, and stop paying his debts.

That second move is where I found myself drifting, and I want to be honest about it, because it is the reason I am writing at all. In my own head the thought had quietly become: *this life is fake, so nothing here has real consequences, and the only thing that counts is the judgment afterwards.* It felt like piety. I now think it was the opposite. It is also, word for word, where the modern version of this idea ends up, at a shrug: *if none of this is real, then none of it weighs anything.*

And the Qur'an anticipates that exact word and refuses it. There is a prayer recorded from people who reasoned their way toward God by looking at the sky: *"Our Lord, You did not create this in vain"* (3:191). The word is **batil**, and it is precisely the word a person reaches for when they say *none of this is real*. It appears in the Book in order to be denied. Elsewhere the denial is flat: *"We did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them in play"* (44:38).

Then the accounting, which is the part that undid my version completely. Not *no consequence* but total consequence, at a resolution no one would choose for themselves: *"So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it"* (99:7-8). A staged room does not keep books like that.

And there is one more thing, which is not an argument but has more force on me than any argument. When his infant son Ibrahim died in his arms, the Prophet ﷺ wept, and said: *"The eye sheds tears and the heart grieves, and we say nothing except what pleases our Lord"* (Bukhari 1303). If the world were a stage set, that grief would have been a category error. It was not a category error. It was the correct response to a real loss inside a world that does not last.

So the accurate sentence is not the one I had been carrying. It is harder and it is better: **this life is short, light, and decisive.** Tiny in size. Infinite in weight. And the shortness is not a permission to sit the thing out; the shortness is the reason to move.

*Which leaves the only question that ever really mattered, and it is not a metaphysical one.*

## III. What survives on a Tuesday morning

Here is what I think the whole business comes down to, and I hold it loosely, because this is the part where I am most likely to be wrong.

If the room is fake, the correct response is contempt, and contempt is cheap. If the room is real but temporary, the correct response is much more demanding: you must treat everything in it seriously, including the people, including the work, including the money, while knowing the whole time that you will set it down. That is a harder posture to hold than either pure worldliness or pure withdrawal, and I suspect that is why so few of us manage it.

The image that helps me is a traveller's grip on his luggage. Firm enough to carry it well, loose enough to put it down when the road turns. Not despising the bag. Not marrying it either.

Practically, for me, it has meant this. That a debt is not less real for being temporary. That an injustice done to someone in a short life is not a small injustice. That work nobody sees still counts, because the accounting is not done by the people who can see it. And that a man may reasonably spend years on something whose result he will not live to enjoy, which is the oldest description of a thing worth building that I know of.

I am aware that a scholar might read the above and find three errors in the first line. That would be a good outcome. It is why it is here.

---

**Bilan.** The suspicion that this life is not the real life is not a wandering thought; it is close to the centre of what Islam teaches, and the Book puts it more strongly than any of us do, calling the next life *the* life and this one a diversion, and measuring a whole lifetime as a day or part of a day. But the conclusion that most people draw from it, the one I drew privately for years, is refused by the same Book in the same breath: creation was not made in vain, it was not made in play, and an atom's weight of anything is seen. **So the answer to the question I began with is that the world is not false; it is simply not final** — and those two are not the same claim at all, and the difference between them is the difference between a man who holds his life lightly and a man who has stopped paying attention to it. **What I am left wondering,** and what I would genuinely like to be argued with about, is what this does to the people around us: if the room is real and temporary, and everyone in it is heading for the same door, does that make the ordinary claims they have on us smaller, or does it make them the only thing here with any weight at all?
