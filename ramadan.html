The Ramadan Room

رَمَضَان

Thirty days that rearrange a year

Ramadan is the ninth month, the one in which the Qur’an was sent down, and the only month named in the Book. This room is built to be worth opening on the third day and on the twenty seventh: what today asks of you, where you are in the reading, what the last ten nights are for, and the plain mechanics nobody quite explains. Every date here is calculated and your masjid’s announcement is the one that counts.

How this room works

This room is open every day of the year and every word in it stays where it is. What changes is the interactive layer. About two weeks before the first of the month the preparation tools wake up and a countdown appears at the top. On the first of Ramadan everything wakes, and today becomes the first thing you see. After Eid the tools go back to resting and the knowledge stays exactly here, so a reader who arrives in October wanting to understand fasting is never turned away at the door.

When a tool is resting it says so plainly, in its own place, and tells you the day it comes back. Nothing is hidden and nothing is disabled without explanation. The whole Codex follows the same calendar: a slim bar appears under the header as the month approaches, the site puts on a slight Ramadan theme for the thirty days, and at Eid the theme changes again and points at the Eid Room.

The dates here are calculated, not sighted. They come from the tabular Islamic calendar, which is arithmetic and has never once looked at the sky, so they can sit a day or two away from the day your community actually begins. Your masjid’s announcement is the date. If it differs from ours, move the calendar with the small offset control below and every time on this page follows you.

Everything else carries its evidence in the open: a verse you can hear recited aloud, a narration named by its collection and number, and an honest badge whenever the scholars differ or a line is our own counsel rather than revelation. Where there is a real disagreement, both sides are given properly, because a reader who is told only one position has been handled rather than taught.

  • The fast begins the moment true dawn enters, not at sunrise. That is why suhoor, the meal before dawn, is eaten late and finished close to the line.
  • It ends the moment the sun is gone, and it is meant to be ended at once. Hastening the iftar is named as a good the community keeps.
  • The night belongs to you. Eat, drink, sleep, and be with your family without guilt: none of that was ever asked of you.

Qur’an 2:187. Eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread, then complete the fast until the night.

All thirty, if you want to read ahead
  1. 1
    The gate opens

    Tonight your body learns what your soul already agreed to. Fasting was written for you as it was written for the people before you, and the reason given is not hunger. It is taqwa, the awareness that He is watching. Begin with that one sentence and the whole month has a floor under it.

  2. 2
    Hunger is a teacher

    By the second day the stomach has stopped negotiating and started talking. Listen to it once, honestly. This is what somebody near you feels on an ordinary Tuesday with no Eid at the end of it. A fast that does not soften you toward the hungry has only made you thinner.

  3. 3
    The mouth fasts too

    Whoever does not abandon false speech and acting upon it, Allah has no need of him abandoning his food and drink. The tongue fasts as well, and it is far harder to hold than the jaw. If somebody picks a fight today, say I am fasting, and let the sentence end there.

  4. 4
    Do not sleep through it

    The month is not a long nap between two meals. Sleep enough to be kind to the people in your house, then spend the hours you saved from cooking and eating on something you will be glad to have done. The days are short and they do not come back.

  5. 5
    Feed one person

    Whoever gives a fasting person something to break his fast with has the same reward as him, and nothing is taken from the fasting person’s own reward. It does not have to be a banquet. A date and a cup of water is named in the report itself.

  6. 6
    Your body has a claim on you

    Salman said to Abu al-Darda: your Lord has a right over you, your body has a right over you, and your family has a right over you, so give each one its right. The Prophet ﷺ heard it and said Salman has spoken the truth. Ramadan is a fast from food in daylight, not a fast from rest, from water at night, or from your family.

  7. 7
    The month of the Book

    The month of Ramadan is the one in which the Qur’an was sent down, guidance for the people. That is the month’s own reason for existing. Read a small amount every single day rather than a heroic amount once, because the small amount is the one that survives to the thirtieth.

  8. 8
    Ask while your hands are open

    And when My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls Me. That verse sits inside the fasting verses, not beside them. Keep a short list and read it in the last minutes before the adhan, when you are already waiting.

  9. 9
    Give before you are asked

    The Prophet ﷺ was the most generous of people, and he was at his most generous in Ramadan, when Jibril would meet him each night to go over the Qur’an with him. Generosity is not only money. It is the seat you gave up, the message you answered, the grudge you quietly put down.

  10. 10
    A third of it is gone

    Ten days have passed and you will not be given them back. Do not audit them with shame; shame is a poor engine and it stalls by the fifteenth. Choose one thing to do better for the next ten, write it somewhere you will actually see it, and leave the first ten alone.

  11. 11
    The prayer nobody sees

    The best prayer after the obligatory ones is the night prayer. It is the one act of worship with no audience at all, which is why it tells the truth about a person. Two units are enough to begin. Two units kept for twenty nights beats twenty units abandoned on the third.

  12. 12
    Forgive one person today

    Let them pardon and overlook. Do you not love that Allah should forgive you? You are spending this month asking to be let off. Ask yourself honestly whether you are holding something over someone that is smaller than what you are asking Him to drop. Let it go today, quietly, without announcing it.

  13. 13
    The white days

    The thirteenth, fourteenth and fifteenth of every month are the white days, named for the full moon that whitens the night. You are already fasting them this month without noticing. Note them now for the eleven months that follow, when fasting will be harder and worth more because nobody else is doing it.

  14. 14
    Halfway, honestly

    Half of it is behind you. Ask one question and answer it in one sentence: has anything actually changed in me, or have I only changed my meal times? If the answer is the second one, there is still half a month left, which is more than most people are given.

  15. 15
    The door with your parents’ name on it

    The parent is the middle door of paradise, so lose that door or guard it. Call them today and stay on the call after the news is finished. If they have died, this is the month to give on their behalf and to ask for them by name, out loud, so your own children hear you do it.

  16. 16
    The strong one

    The strong one is not the one who throws people down. The strong one is the one who holds himself together at the moment of anger. Fasting takes away your usual comforts on purpose, which means every irritation arrives undefended. That is not the month failing. That is the training.

  17. 17
    Badr

    On the seventeenth of Ramadan in the second year after the migration, a little over three hundred fasting men stood at Badr against three times their number, with the wrong equipment and no line of retreat. Allah had already given you victory at Badr while you were weak. Whatever you are afraid of today has been faced by someone with less.

  18. 18
    The plate over the wall

    Jibril kept counselling me about the neighbour until I thought he would make him an heir. Ramadan is a loud month of cooking smells travelling along a corridor. Somewhere near you is a flat that smells of nothing. Send a plate, without a note, and do not wait to be thanked.

  19. 19
    Clear the ground

    The last ten nights begin the night after tomorrow. Do the shopping now. Buy the gifts now. Settle the travel and the family arrangements now. It is a quiet tragedy to spend the most valuable nights of your year standing in a queue holding a basket.

  20. 20
    Tonight the last ten begin

    When the last ten entered, the Prophet ﷺ would tighten his waist wrapper, stay awake through the night, and wake his family. Scholars have read that tightening in more than one way. However it is read, the plain meaning stands: something changes tonight, in the house as well as in the person.

  21. 21
    The first odd night

    Seek it in the odd nights of the last ten. Tonight is the first of them. Nobody was told which night it is, and that hiding is deliberate: a person who knew the date would worship one night and sleep nine. You were made to search all ten.

  22. 22
    Better than a thousand months

    A thousand months is eighty three years and four months, which is a whole human life. One night has been placed inside your month that is worth more than all of it. Even the arithmetic alone should make a reasonable person sit up and rearrange the evening.

  23. 23
    The night some were shown

    Abdullah ibn Unays was shown it on the twenty third and prayed that night in water and mud. Ubayy ibn Ka’b swore by Allah that it was the twenty seventh. The reports do not agree, and the strongest reading of that disagreement is that the night moves from year to year. So come to all of them.

  24. 24
    What to ask for

    Aisha asked what she should say if she found the night, and she was not taught to ask for things. She was taught to ask to be pardoned. Ask for the largest thing first. Then ask for your small ones as well, by name, because He is not troubled by the length of a list.

  25. 25
    The tiredness lies

    Around the twenty fifth the body files a convincing report saying it has done enough and the important nights are behind it. The report is wrong on both counts. Do not let tiredness make a decision that your whole year depends on.

  26. 26
    The eve of the twenty seventh

    Tonight is the twenty seventh night on the reckoning most communities follow, and the masjid will be fuller than it has been all year. Go early enough to get a place. Take your children. Sit where they can see something happening, and let them remember the crowd.

  27. 27
    The night most sought

    The word qadr carries both meanings at once: the measuring out of what will be, and sheer worth. On it every wise matter is made distinct, by a command from Us. Whatever else you do tonight, do not hand it to a screen that will give you nothing back.

  28. 28
    Two nights left

    Deeds are judged by their endings. Two nights remain, and depending on whether this month runs twenty nine days or thirty, one of them is odd numbered in a reckoning you cannot see. Spend both as though the night you have been looking for is the one in front of you.

  29. 29
    Pay it before the prayer

    It must be in the hands of the poor before the Eid prayer, not after it. Paid after, it is an ordinary charity and no longer this obligation. Pay for every soul in your house, including the child born last week and the guest who is staying with you. If your masjid is collecting, hand it in today.

  30. 30
    Do not put the lamp out

    Whoever fasts Ramadan and then follows it with six days of Shawwal, it is as though he fasted the whole year. The month is ending. The person it built does not have to end with it. Keep one thing, a small one, and carry it into an ordinary week where nobody is watching.

الاسْتِعْدَاد

Getting ready

al-istidad, the making ready

Ramadan is not a month you can start well on the first morning. Ten things, none of them heavy, all of them easier now than they will be later. The checklist below wakes about two weeks before the month; the ten things themselves are readable in October, in June, and on any other day you happen to be here.

The preparation checklist opens two weeks before Ramadan.The ten things are printed in full just below, and they are worth reading whenever you are here.

Your preparation

0 of 10 done

The ten, in full
  1. 1
    Pay back the fasts you still owe

    Days missed last Ramadan for illness, travel or menstruation are a debt, and they are meant to be cleared before the next month starts. Aisha used to make hers up in Shaban for exactly this reason. If there are too many to finish now, start anyway.

  2. 2
    Decide the reading, and decide where the Book will sit

    One juz a night finishes the Qur’an in thirty. Pick the copy you will actually use, put it somewhere you pass four times a day, and open the plan below so the nights are already mapped before the first one arrives.

  3. 3
    Move your sleep now, not on the first night

    The single most common way a first week gets wrecked. Shift bedtime earlier by twenty minutes every couple of days starting this week and the suhoor alarm stops being a shock.

  4. 4
    Ask your masjid for this year’s zakat al-fitr figure

    They publish it, they know the local staple and its price, and it is owed for every soul under your roof. Set the money aside now while it is easy, then use the reckoner below so nothing is short at the end.

  5. 5
    Book the last ten nights if you can

    Leave, shifts, childcare. Whatever it is, it is booked in advance or it does not happen. The night that is better than a thousand months is somewhere in there.

  6. 6
    Settle what you owe, and let go of what you are owed

    Money first, because it is concrete. Then the harder one: the person you have not spoken to. Walking into the month with that unresolved makes every night of it smaller.

  7. 7
    Tell the children what is coming, and give each one a job

    Waking the house for suhoor, laying the dates out, counting the nights on a chart. A child with a job in Ramadan remembers Ramadan. A child with no job remembers being hungry and bored.

  8. 8
    Stock for suhoor, not only for iftar

    Almost every household over buys for the evening and forgets the meal that actually carries the fast. Oats, eggs, dates, yoghurt, water. Boring on purpose.

  9. 9
    Pick one thing to drop and one thing to add

    One only, of each. Thirty days is long enough to break a habit and short enough that a list of ten will collapse by the fourth night.

  10. 10
    Learn the words you will say when you break it

    Two short lines, said thousands of times over your life. Learning them in the week before costs an hour and is paid back every single evening of the month.

خَتْمَة

The Qur’an in thirty nights

khatma, one complete reading from the first word to the last

The Book was divided into thirty parts for exactly this month. One part a night, about twenty pages, and on the thirtieth you close it having read every word. Tick the nights as you go. Nothing leaves this browser and nobody is counting but you.

  • Juz, the plural is ajza, means a part. The Qur’an was divided into thirty of them for exactly this: one a night, and the month closes the Book.
  • Twenty pages sounds heavy until it is broken up. Four pages after each of the five daily prayers is the same thing, and it never takes more than a few minutes at a time.

العَشْر الأَوَاخِر

The last ten nights

al-ashr al-awakhir

The month has a summit and it is at the end. From the night that follows the twentieth day, the Prophet ﷺ changed how he lived, and so should you.

  • The lit lanterns are the odd nights counted from the beginning: the twenty first, twenty third, twenty fifth, twenty seventh and twenty ninth.
  • Counted backward from the end they land differently, and nobody outside knows yet whether this month has twenty nine days or thirty. That is the whole argument for giving all ten.
  • The twenty seventh is the most reported and it is not the certain one. Treat it as the likeliest, not the only.

Bukhari 2017. Seek the Night of Decree in the odd nights of the last ten of Ramadan.

What the night is

Qur’an

Indeed We sent it down on the Night of Decree. And what can make you know what the Night of Decree is? The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months. The angels and the Spirit descend in it by the permission of their Lord for every matter. Peace it is, until the rise of dawn.

Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Decree, carries two meanings in its own name. Qadr is the measuring out of what will be, and qadr is worth. A thousand months is eighty three years and four months, longer than most lives. One night is placed inside your month that outweighs it.

Qur’anWe sent it down on the Night of DecreeQur’anbetter than a thousand monthsQur’anpeace, until the rise of dawnQur’ansent down on a blessed night

Where to look for it

Sunnah

Seek it in the odd nights of the last ten. That is the instruction, and it is the whole instruction. The Prophet ﷺ came out to tell the Companions the exact night, found two men quarrelling, and the knowledge of it was taken up. He said: perhaps that is better for you. Seek it in the ninth, the seventh and the fifth of the last ten.

Some Companions were shown it on the twenty third. Ubayy ibn Ka’b swore by Allah, with no exception made, that it was the twenty seventh. Both reports are sound. Most scholars read the disagreement the honest way: the night moves between years, and the twenty seventh is the likeliest single night without being the certain one.

There is a practical mercy in the odd nights being counted two ways. Nobody standing outside a masjid knows whether this month will run twenty nine days or thirty, so what is odd from the beginning may be even from the end. The safe answer, and the answer the Prophet ﷺ lived, is all ten.

What it is worth to stand it

Sunnah

Whoever stands the Night of Decree in faith and seeking the reward will have what came before of his sin forgiven. The two conditions are stated and they are small: believe it is true, and want the reward from Allah rather than from anyone watching.

Standing does not mean an unbroken night of prayer for a person who has work at seven. It means the night is given a share it did not have yesterday: the Isha prayer and the taraweeh with the congregation, then whatever you can hold of Qur’an, du’a and witr before you sleep.

Bukhari 1901in faith and seeking reward, what came before is forgivenMuslim 760the same wording through a second chain

The du’a Aisha was taught

Sunnah

Aisha asked: Messenger of Allah, if I know which night is the Night of Decree, what should I say in it? He said: say, O Allah, You are Pardoning and You love to pardon, so pardon me.

Notice what she was not taught to ask for. Not wealth, not ease, not even paradise directly. She was taught to ask for afw, the pardon that wipes a thing away so completely that it is not even brought up. Ask for that first. Then ask for your own small list too, out loud and by name, because a long list does not tire Him.

اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّكَ عَفُوٌّ تُحِبُّ الْعَفْوَ فَاعْفُ عَنِّي

Allahumma innaka afuwwun tuhibbu al-afwa fa’fu anni

O Allah, You are Pardoning, You love to pardon, so pardon me.

Some routes of the same narration add the word karim, generous, after afuwwun. Both wordings are in circulation and neither one is wrong to say. The wording above is the one in Tirmidhi’s text.

I’tikaf for people who have to work

Scholars differ

I’tikaf means keeping yourself in the masjid, cut off from ordinary business, for the sake of Allah. The Prophet ﷺ did it every year in the last ten, and in the year he died he did twenty days. Aisha reported that his wives kept it after him. It is a confirmed sunnah, not an obligation, and nobody is sinful for missing it.

The scholars differ on the smallest amount that counts. The Hanafi school holds that the sunnah i’tikaf of the last ten requires the full ten with the nights. The Shafi’i school allows a very short stay, even part of an hour, to count as a valid i’tikaf. The Maliki and Hanbali positions sit between, with a day and a night commonly named as the least that is meaningful. If you follow a school, follow it. If you do not, know that a shorter i’tikaf is a real position held by real scholars and not a soft excuse invented last week.

So build the ten nights around the job instead of pretending the job is not there. Pray Isha and taraweeh at the masjid and stay after it. Take the odd nights as leave if you can take leave at all, and make the twenty seventh the one you protect. Go in with your phone off and left in a bag, because a body in the masjid and a mind in a feed is not i’tikaf.

Women may keep i’tikaf; the wives of the Prophet ﷺ did, in the masjid, with his permission. Some later jurists preferred a woman’s i’tikaf in a set place at home. That is a scholarly preference and not a text, and it should be named as what it is. Decide it with your own family, and with a scholar you actually trust.

Bukhari 2026he kept i’tikaf in the last ten until Allah took himBukhari 2041his wives kept i’tikaf after himQur’ando not touch your wives while you are in retreat in the masjids

A plan for the ten that a tired person can keep

Editorial

Decide the ten nights before the twentieth, not during. Write down which nights you will give in full, which you will give an hour, and who is covering the children on each of them. A plan made while you are rested survives a night when you are not.

Front load the sleep. A short sleep after Isha, before you stand, is older than any productivity advice and it is what makes the last third of the night possible at all. And take the meal seriously: a heavy iftar is the most common reason a person means to stand and does not.

التَّرَاوِيح

Taraweeh, the standing of the month

at-tarawih

The night prayer of Ramadan, prayed in company. Its name comes from raha, rest, because the early people sat down to rest after every four units. What is established is clear; the number of units is an old and civil disagreement.

  • No text fixes a number for the night prayer. That is why the disagreement is old, wide and calm, and why none of the three positions calls the others wrong.
  • The strongest single description of the Prophet’s ﷺ own night prayer is Aisha’s eleven. The strongest public practice of the generation after him is twenty.
  • Pray with the imam of the masjid you are standing in. Whoever stands with him until he leaves is written as having stood the whole night.

Bukhari 1147, Bukhari 2012 and Tirmidhi 806. A difference of arrangement, not of religion.

What is established

Sunnah

Whoever stands in Ramadan out of faith and seeking the reward will be forgiven what came before of his sin. That is the promise, and it is attached to the standing itself, not to any particular count.

The Prophet ﷺ prayed it in the masjid on three nights and the people gathered behind him. On the fourth night the masjid could not hold them, and he did not come out. In the morning he said: I feared it would be made obligatory upon you and you would be unable to carry it. So he stopped for their sake, not because the prayer was disliked.

After him, Umar found the people praying scattered, a man alone and a man with a few behind him, and he gathered them behind Ubayy ibn Ka’b. Walking through the masjid another night and seeing them in one row he said: what an excellent innovation this is. He meant new in its arrangement, not new in its origin, since the Prophet ﷺ had prayed it himself and only stopped for fear of it becoming compulsory.

The number of units, stated fairly

Scholars differ

Aisha was asked how the Prophet ﷺ prayed in Ramadan. She said he did not exceed eleven units in Ramadan or outside it, and she described four, then four, then three. That report is in Bukhari and Muslim and it is the strongest single description of his own night prayer.

The report of Umar’s gathering reaches us in more than one wording. One route in Malik’s Muwatta names eleven. Other routes, and the settled practice of Madinah and Kufa in the generation after, name twenty followed by three of witr. Twenty is what the Hanafi, Shafi’i and Hanbali schools came to, and it is what most masjids pray. The people of Madinah later prayed thirty six, adding rounds in place of the rest between the sets, and Malik saw no harm in it.

So here are the positions without a thumb on the scale. Those who hold to eleven argue from Aisha’s report and say that a described sunnah is not improved by addition. Those who hold to twenty argue from the practice of the Companions and the generations after them, which was public, in Madinah, and uncontested at the time, and from the fact that no text limits the number of night prayer units at all.

Ibn Taymiyya, who cannot be accused of leniency, wrote that the matter is wide: whoever prays twenty, as most do, has done well; whoever prays thirty nine has done well; whoever prays eleven or thirteen has done well. If your masjid prays twenty and you prefer eleven, pray with them and sit out the rest quietly, or pray eight and stay for the witr. Do not turn a night of mercy into an argument in the shoe rack.

Bukhari 1147Aisha: he did not exceed eleven, in Ramadan or outside itMuslim 738the same report of eleven unitsMuwatta Malik 250the routes naming eleven and naming twenty threeIbn Taymiyya Majmu al-Fatawa 22the matter is wide; twenty, thirty nine or eleven are all well

Stay until the imam finishes

Sunnah

Whoever stands with the imam until he leaves, it is written for him as though he stood the whole night. That single sentence answers the question of whether to leave after eight units when the imam is praying twenty. Stay, and you are credited with a night you did not have to give.

There is a second reason to pray it in company, and it has nothing to do with reward arithmetic. A person praying alone in a room in the twenty fourth night of a hard month will find reasons to stop. A row of shoulders will carry you when your own intention will not.

If the masjid is far, or shut, or not for you tonight

Editorial

The congregation is better and it is not a condition. If you are unwell, if you are alone with small children, if the only masjid is an hour away, then pray it at home. A woman praying at home has the reward of the prayer, and a man kept away by a real obstacle is not sinning.

Pray as a household if you can. One of you leads, however plainly, and the others stand behind. Children who see a prayer begin in their own living room will remember Ramadan as something their family did, not something a building did.

الصِّيَام

Fasting in practice

as-siyam

The plain mechanics of the fast, the excuses Allah Himself wrote into the verses, and the small mercies people are most often surprised to learn are there.

  • The excuses are not loopholes found by lawyers. They are written into the verse, in the same breath as the command.
  • The one who is ill or travelling makes the days up later, one for one, at any time before the next Ramadan.
  • The one who will never be able, through age or lasting illness, feeds a poor person for each day instead. That is fidya, the ransom.

Qur’an 2:184 and 2:185. Whoever among you is ill or on a journey, then a number of other days. Allah intends ease for you and does not intend hardship for you.

Who fasts

Qur’an

O you who believe, fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may attain taqwa. Then: whoever among you witnesses the month, let him fast it.

The obligation falls on the Muslim who is adult, sane, settled and able. A child is not obliged until puberty. The Companions still had their children fast and made them woollen toys, so that when one of them cried for food they gave him the toy until sunset. Train them; do not burden them.

Qur’anprescribed for you as for those before youQur’anwhoever witnesses the month, let him fast itBukhari 1960the children of the Companions and the woollen toys

Who is excused, and what they owe instead

Qur’an

Whoever is ill or on a journey, then a number of other days. Allah intends ease for you and does not intend hardship for you. The excuse is in the Book itself, and taking it is obedience, not weakness.

And upon those who can fast only with hardship, a ransom of feeding a poor person. That is fidya, the ransom: for the permanently ill and the very old who will not recover a lost day, one poor person fed for each day missed.

A woman who is menstruating or in postnatal bleeding does not fast, and she makes up the days later. That is not a deficiency in her; it is a rule given to her. She does not make up the prayers, only the fasts.

Pregnancy and nursing are where the schools genuinely split. Some hold she makes up the days like a sick person. Some hold she pays the ransom and feeds instead. Some hold she does both if she broke the fast out of fear for the child rather than for herself. All three are held by serious scholars. Ask the one your family follows, decide once, and stop carrying the question around.

Qur’ana number of other days; a ransom of feeding a poor personQur’anAllah intends ease for youBukhari 1951making up a fast on behalf of the deadMuslim 335she makes up the fast and not the prayer

The intention

Scholars differ

Deeds are by intentions, and every person has what he intended. The intention of a fast is a resolve in the heart. There is no formula to recite, and the sentences printed on Ramadan calendars are a custom, not a sunnah.

The majority hold that the obligatory fast needs its intention formed at night, before dawn. The Maliki school allows one intention at the start of the month to cover all of it, which is a real mercy for anyone who sometimes wakes late. Getting up for suhoor is itself an intention, since nobody eats at three in the morning by accident.

Bukhari 1deeds are by intentionsNasa’i 2333no fast for one who did not intend it in the night; graded differently by the scholars

What breaks the fast

Sunnah

Eating and drinking on purpose. Sexual intercourse. Making yourself vomit. The arrival of menstrual or postnatal bleeding, at any hour of the day.

Intercourse during a day of Ramadan is the one that carries a heavy expiation: freeing a slave, or fasting two consecutive months, or feeding sixty poor people. The man who came to the Prophet ﷺ saying he was ruined was given that scale, and when he could do none of it he was given dates to feed others with, and then told to feed his own family with them when he said nobody in the city was poorer than him. Even the expiation was answered with mercy.

Everything else is measured by that list, not added to it freely. A rule that keeps people from a fast they could have kept needs evidence, not a feeling.

Qur’aneat and drink until the white thread is distinct from the blackBukhari 1936the man who came saying he was ruinedAbu Dawud 2380whoever is overcome by vomiting owes nothing

What does not break it, though people think it does

Sunnah

Forgetting and eating. Whoever forgets he is fasting and eats or drinks, let him complete his fast, for it was Allah who fed him and gave him drink. Do not spit it out in a panic and do not start the day again. Finish the mouthful you were already chewing, then carry on.

Vomiting that overtakes you. Brushing your teeth, and the siwak, the tooth stick, which the Prophet ﷺ used while fasting. Rinsing the mouth and nose in wudu, though without going deep, since that instruction was given to the fasting person specifically. Swallowing your own saliva. Tasting food on the tongue and spitting it out, which Ibn Abbas saw no harm in. Blood tests. A shower, or swimming, or standing under cold water for relief, which the Prophet ﷺ did.

Waking up in a state of major impurity does not spoil the fast; the Prophet ﷺ would reach dawn in that state and fast the day. A wet dream while fasting does not break it either, since it is not chosen.

Injections and inhalers are modern questions and the scholars are genuinely divided. The common line drawn by contemporary councils is that what nourishes breaks the fast and what does not nourish does not, so a vaccine or an antibiotic injection is treated differently from a drip of nutrients. If you depend on an inhaler to breathe, use it, and ask a scholar afterwards rather than risking your lungs to a question you can settle in the evening.

Bukhari 1933Allah fed him and gave him drinkMuslim 1155the same reportAbu Dawud 2366be thorough in rinsing unless you are fastingBukhari 1926he would reach dawn in a state of impurity and fast

Suhoor, and hastening the iftar

Sunnah

Take suhoor, the meal before dawn, for in suhoor there is baraka, blessing. It is not merely fuel. Zayd ibn Thabit measured the gap between the Prophet’s ﷺ suhoor and the prayer at about the length of fifty verses, which is to say: eat late, close to the line, not at midnight.

At the other end, do not hold on. The people will remain upon good as long as they hasten the breaking of the fast. He would break it before praying Maghrib, with fresh dates, or dried dates, or a few mouthfuls of water if there were none. There is no virtue in adding minutes that were never asked of you.

The words reported at the moment of breaking are simple: dhahaba al-zama, wabtallat al-uruq, wa thabata al-ajr insha Allah. The thirst has gone, the veins are moist, and the reward is fixed, if Allah wills.

If you break it, or miss a day

Editorial

A day missed for a real excuse is made up later, one for one, at any time before the next Ramadan. There is no order to keep them consecutive. Aisha used to make up her days in Shaban, the month before, and nobody criticised her for the delay.

A day broken deliberately with no excuse is a serious matter and it is not beyond repair. Make it up, ask for forgiveness plainly, and do not let one broken day become an abandoned month. The one who repents from a sin is like the one who has no sin at all.

Bukhari 1950Aisha making up her days in ShabanIbn Majah 4250the one who repents is like one with no sin; graded hasan
زَكَاة الفِطْر

Zakat al-Fitr

zakat al-fitr

A small obligatory charity, paid in food or its value for every soul in the house, that must arrive before the Eid prayer. It is the last thing the month asks of you and the easiest to forget.

  • One sa’ of the staple food per person, not per family. The newborn counts. The guest sleeping in the spare room counts.
  • A sa’ is a volume of four cupped handfuls, which lands near three kilograms depending on what is measured, which is why the figure your masjid quotes moves a little.
  • The deadline is the prayer, not the day. Give it early if you can and stop carrying it.

Bukhari 1503 and Abu Dawud 1609. A purification for the fasting person and food for the poor: whoever pays it before the prayer, it is accepted zakat.

What it is for

Sunnah

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ made zakat al-fitr obligatory as a purification for the fasting person from idle talk and indecency, and as food for the poor. It has two purposes and both are stated in one sentence: it cleans up the month you just kept, and it puts food on a table that would otherwise be bare on the one morning of the year when nobody should be hungry.

It is due from every Muslim who has more than his own household’s food for that day and night. It is not a tax on the wealthy. Almost everyone owes it.

Abu Dawud 1609a purification for the fasting person and food for the poorIbn Majah 1827the same report

The measure

Sunnah

Ibn Umar said: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ made it obligatory, one sa’ of dates or one sa’ of barley, upon the slave and the free, the male and the female, the young and the old among the Muslims, and ordered that it be paid before the people went out to the prayer.

A sa’ is a volume, not a weight: four cupped handfuls of an average adult. In weight it works out at roughly two and a half to three kilograms depending on what is being measured, which is why masjids quote a slightly different figure for wheat than for rice. Abu Sa’id al-Khudri listed what they used to give: a sa’ of food, or barley, or dates, or dried yoghurt, or raisins. The principle is the staple food of the place, not a particular grain.

Bukhari 1503one sa’, upon every soul among the MuslimsBukhari 1506Abu Sa’id listing what they gaveMuslim 985the same list through a second route

Food or its value in money

Scholars differ

This is the live disagreement and it deserves to be stated straight. The Hanafi school permits paying the value in cash, arguing that the aim named in the text is feeding the poor, and money buys what a household actually needs. The Maliki, Shafi’i and Hanbali schools hold to food itself, arguing that the Prophet ﷺ specified food while money existed in Madinah, and a specified act of worship is not swapped for a convenience.

Neither side is careless. In practice, most masjids outside the Muslim world collect cash and buy staples in bulk, which satisfies both readings at once: you hand over money, food arrives at a door. If that is what your masjid does, give through them and the question does not arise for you.

Bukhari 1503the text names food by measureThe four schoolsHanafi permits value; Maliki, Shafi’i and Hanbali hold to food

Who pays for whom

Sunnah

The head of a household pays for himself and for everyone he is responsible for: his wife, his children including one born before dawn on the night of Eid, and any dependent living under his roof. It is one sa’ per person, not one per family.

It is given to the poor and the needy. The classic advice is to keep it near: the poor of your own city have the first claim, and a relative in need is both a charity and a keeping of the ties of kinship. Where you cannot find need locally, giving through a trustworthy body that moves it quickly is fine, as long as it moves before the prayer.

Bukhari 1503upon the young and the old, the male and the femaleAbu Dawud 1609food for the poor

When it must arrive

Sunnah

Before the Eid prayer. That is the deadline in the text and it is not decorative: whoever pays it before the prayer, it is an accepted zakat, and whoever pays it after the prayer, it is a charity among charities. The obligation itself is missed.

Paying early is fine and was done. Ibn Umar used to give it a day or two before Eid, and the Companions would pay it one or two days ahead. If your masjid collects from the twentieth night, pay then and stop carrying it. The commonest failure is a person who fully intended to pay and was still intending it while the imam said the takbir.

عِيد الفِطْر

Eid al-Fitr

Eid al-Fitr

The festival of the breaking of the fast, on the first day of Shawwal. A day of thanks with a shape to it, and the shape is worth keeping.

  • The prayer is after the sun has risen and lifted clear, in the open where the whole community can be seen at once. No call to prayer is made for it.
  • Dates are eaten before leaving, in an odd number, on Eid al-Fitr alone. On Eid al-Adha the order is reversed and nothing is eaten until the return.
  • He went out by one road and came back by another. Take the long way and let the children notice.

Bukhari 953, Bukhari 960 and Bukhari 986. Two units with no call and no iqama, dates before setting out, and a different road home.

The morning

Sunnah

Wash. Ibn Umar would perform ghusl, a full bath, on the morning of Eid before going out to the prayer place, and the practice is reported from a number of the Companions. Then wear the best clothes you own. They do not have to be new, and nothing in the sunnah asks you to buy anything.

Eat before you leave, and only on this Eid. The Prophet ﷺ would not go out on the morning of Eid al-Fitr until he had eaten some dates, and he would eat them in an odd number. On Eid al-Adha the order is reversed: he would not eat until he returned. The eating before the prayer on this day is a small public statement that the fast is genuinely over.

Go by one road and come back by another. He did that, and the scholars have read it every way from greeting more people to letting two streets witness for you. Take the children the long way round and let it be the strange thing they remember.

Bukhari 953he would not go out until he ate dates, an odd numberBukhari 986he would return by a different roadMuwatta Malik 428Ibn Umar bathing on the morning of Eid

The takbir

Qur’an

He wants for you ease and does not want for you hardship, and so that you may complete the number and magnify Allah for having guided you, and so that you may be thankful. The takbir on the way to the prayer is that verse being obeyed out loud.

Say it from the night the moon of Shawwal is announced until the imam comes out for the prayer. Say it walking, in the car, on the stairs, and say it so children can hear the sound of a crowd that is not chanting for a team.

The best known wording comes from Ibn Mas’ud and other Companions. There are several reported variants and none of them is a battleground.

اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، وَلِلَّهِ الْحَمْدُ

Allah is greater, Allah is greater. There is no god but Allah. And Allah is greater, Allah is greater, and to Allah belongs all praise.

The prayer

Sunnah

Two units, prayed in congregation, in the morning after the sun has risen and lifted clear of the horizon. There is no adhan and no iqama for it. Jabir said plainly: there was no call and no iqama on the day of Eid.

Extra takbirs are said before the recitation: in the well known practice, seven in the first unit including the opening one, and five in the second. The counts differ a little between the schools and none of the differences invalidates anyone’s prayer. Follow the imam and do not count out loud.

The sermon comes after the prayer, not before it, which is the opposite of Friday. Sitting for it is the strong sunnah though it is not obligatory, and walking out of it while the imam is speaking is the small rudeness that spoils a good morning.

Everyone goes out. He commanded that the young women, the women in seclusion, and the menstruating women be brought out, so that they might witness the good and the gathering of the believers, and the menstruating women keep away from the prayer itself. A masjid that quietly leaves half its people at home on Eid morning has departed from the sunnah, not preserved it.

What to say to each other

Editorial

The Companions would say to one another, when they met on the day of Eid: taqabbal Allahu minna wa minkum. May Allah accept from us and from you. Ibn Hajar records the chain as acceptable. It is a better sentence than a generic greeting because it asks for something.

Then do the ordinary things properly. Visit the relative you have been avoiding. Give the children money and let them keep it. Eat in daylight without flinching. And check who is alone: Eid is the loneliest morning of the year for a convert with no family in the city, and one invitation fixes it.

Keeping children in it, joyfully

al-usra fi Ramadan

A child’s first Ramadan memory is set young and it is almost impossible to reset. Make it light, loud and warm, and the fasting will arrive on its own.

How the Companions did it

Sunnah

Ar-Rubayyi bint Mu’awwidh described it: we would fast and we would have our little children fast, and we would make them a toy of wool. When one of them cried for food we would give him the toy until it was time for the iftar.

Two things are in that report. They involved the children, and they distracted the children. Neither shouting nor a lecture appears anywhere in it. A half day fast, a countdown, a job to do at sunset: those are the tools, and they are eleven hundred years older than any parenting book.

Bukhari 1960the woollen toy given until iftarMuslim 1136the same report

Things that actually work

Editorial

Decorate. Lanterns in a hallway cost very little and they tell a four year old that something important is happening. A paper chain with thirty links, one torn off each evening, does more than any explanation.

Give every child a job at iftar: the one who pours the water, the one who counts out the dates, the one who calls everyone to the table. A job outranks a rule.

Let them break a half day fast at noon with a small celebration, and mean it. Do not let the first fast be a test they can fail.

Read one short surah aloud together before the food, every night, the same one for a week. By the twenty ninth they will be saying it with you without having been asked to memorise anything.

Take them to the masjid at night at least once, even if they last twenty minutes. The noise, the shoes, the crowd and the late hour is the memory. Nobody remembers a lesson; everyone remembers being out past bedtime with their father.
