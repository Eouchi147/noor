You Just Became New

For converts and reverts: what actually just happened, what to do in your first day and your first forty, and how to survive the wobbles, without pretending you grew up with this.

You are not a beginner at being loved by Allah

١ What your shahada actually did

You said two sentences, ash-hadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasul Allah, and according to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ himself, everything before them was wiped from your record. Not archived. Wiped. When Amr ibn al-As came to pledge allegiance and hesitated over his past, the Prophet ﷺ told him: "Do you not know that Islam demolishes what came before it?" (Muslim 121).

So the person your old mistakes belonged to no longer exists in the ledger. You are, in the most literal bookkeeping sense, the cleanest adult you know. Many lifelong Muslims will tell you, with a little envy, that they wish they had your page.

قُلْ يَا عِبَادِيَ الَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا عَلَىٰ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا مِن رَّحْمَةِ اللَّهِ

"Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah."

Qur'an · Az-Zumar 39:53

٢ Your first 24 hours

1

Breathe. Nothing is due yet. There is no exam tonight. The obligations enter your life the way dawn enters a room, gradually and on schedule, not all at once.

2

Learn one sentence: Bismillah ("in the name of Allah"), say it before you eat, drink, or begin anything. That's a complete act of worship. You're already practicing.

3

Take a full wash (ghusl) if you haven't, a simple full-body rinse with the intention of purification. A shower with intention counts.

4

Tell one safe person, or no one. There is no obligation to announce. Your safety and stability matter in Islamic law; scholars across the schools permit a new Muslim to keep their Islam private while they find their feet.

5

Sleep like someone forgiven, because that is the technical status of your account.

٣ Your first forty days, a gentle ladder

The minimum is smaller than you fear; the maximum is larger than you need. Here is a humane pace, not a legal schedule, just a proven one:

Wk 1

Wudu + the motions. Learn ablution and physically walk through the prayer positions with a video or a friend, no Arabic required yet. Pray along in English while you learn; understanding precedes recitation.

Wk 2

Al-Fatiha, one line a day. Seven short lines, learn one per day with the Mushaf's recitation on repeat. By Friday you own the spine of the prayer for life.

Wk 3

Anchor two prayers, most people start with Fajr and Isha (home prayers). Two anchored beats five attempted-and-collapsed. Add the others one at a time as they stick.

Wk 4-5

Add Surah al-Ikhlas + the two refuges (112, 113, 114, twelve short verses total) and the closing tashahhud. Your prayer is now fully self-sufficient.

Wk 6

Enter a masjid on a Friday. Stand at the back, copy the room. Nobody is grading you; half the room is worried about their own khushu'.

The minimum & the reasonable maximum

Minimum required: the five prayers (learned progressively), Ramadan's fast when it arrives (exemptions apply), honesty, and avoiding the clearly forbidden. That's the floor, and the floor is enough. Reasonable maximum: add the Sunnah gradually, extra prayers, Monday/Thursday fasts, nightly Qur'an, only at a pace you can keep for years. The Prophet ﷺ warned against burning bright and burning out: "This religion is ease, and no one makes it severe except that it defeats him" (Bukhari 39).

٤ The wobbles, every convert has them

"The spark faded"

Around month two or three, the honeymoon light dims. This is so universal the Prophet ﷺ named it: every deed has an initial zeal (shirrah) then a lull (fatrah), and "whoever's lull stays within my Sunnah has succeeded" (Ahmad 6764 · sahih). The lull is not apostasy. It's the second stage of faith.

"I'm alone in this"

Loneliness is the convert's heaviest weight, old circle drifting, new circle not yet grown. Go where Muslims are, even silently: Jumu'ah, a weekly class, one WhatsApp group. Community is not a luxury in this religion; the prayer itself was designed in rows.

"My family is grieving me"

Parents may mourn, mock, or rage. The Qur'an's instruction is surgical: even if they strive to pull you back to shirk, do not obey them, and keep their company in this world with kindness (Luqman 31:15). Firm faith, soft hands. Decades of convert experience say: let your character argue for you; it's the only da'wah most families can hear.

"I keep sinning"

You didn't leave sin behind at the shahada; you left despair. "All the children of Adam constantly err, and the best of those who err are the constant repenters" (Tirmidhi 2499 · hasan). Repentance is not the emergency exit of this religion. It's the front door, used daily, by everyone.

٥ The questions everyone actually asks

Do I have to change my name?

No, not unless your name carries a meaning that contradicts Islam (rare). The companions kept their pre-Islamic names in almost every case; the Prophet ﷺ changed only names with bad meanings. Your name, your family, your face: all still yours.

What about my non-Muslim family, can I still love them, visit, care for them?

You must. Kindness to parents is commanded in the same verses that command tawhid (Al-Isra 17:23), and Luqman 31:15 commands beautiful companionship even to parents who oppose your Islam. Being Muslim makes you a better child, not a stranger.

I can't read Arabic. Am I a second-class Muslim?

Bilal, the first mu'adhdhin, was Abyssinian; Salman was Persian; Suhayb was Roman. The Qur'an calls itself Arabic; it never calls its people that. Learn Al-Fatiha by sound (everyone did, including native speakers as children), and read meaning in your own language without guilt, the Mushaf here keeps translation one tap away.

What if I converted for someone, marriage, a friend?

The door you came through matters less than the house you're in. Many of the ummah's brightest lights entered for one reason and stayed for another. Renew the intention now, in one breath, and it's yours alone.

Do I have to be perfect at halal food / dress immediately?

The Qur'an itself descended over 23 years; the prohibition of wine alone came in stages. Fix the prayer first, it's the spine that straightens everything else. Then move one thing at a time. Gradualism isn't weakness; it's the revealed method.

Where do I learn without getting radicalized or overwhelmed online?

Three rules: prefer teachers with real-world mosques and classical training over anonymous accounts; prefer the middle voice over the loudest one; and measure any teaching against the Prophet's ﷺ actual character: "I was only sent to perfect noble character" (Ahmad 8952 · sahih). Start local: your nearest masjid's new-Muslim class outweighs a thousand debate videos.
