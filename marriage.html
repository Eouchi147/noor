A room of the Codex

النِّكَاح

Marriage & the Home

What nikah actually is, how to choose, what the contract says, how the years are lived, and how a storm is weathered. Graded by its evidence, like every room in this house.

One

What nikah is

المِيثَاق

al-mithaq · Before the dresses and the halls, marriage is a word the Qur'an reserves for almost nothing else.

Qur’an

A heavy covenant

The Qur'an calls marriage a mithaq ghaliz, a heavy covenant (4:21). The same weight of phrase is used for the pact Allah took from the prophets. That is the register: not a party, not a transaction between families, but a solemn undertaking witnessed before Allah.

Because it is a covenant, it has terms, witnesses, and consequences. Islam did not romanticise marriage into vagueness; it wrote it down.

Qur’an 4:21
Qur’an

The sign planted in it

Among His signs is that He created for you spouses from among yourselves so that you may find rest in them, and He placed between you love and mercy (30:21). Three words carry the verse: sukun, rest; mawadda, warm love; rahma, mercy.

Notice the order of the years: the love that begins a marriage is named, and so is the mercy that keeps one going long after the beginning has grown quiet.

Qur’an 30:21
Editorial

Why they call it half the deen

A widely quoted saying calls marriage half of religion. Its chains are graded differently by the scholars, so this room will not print it as certainty. The meaning it points at, though, stands on firm ground: the Prophet ﷺ told the young that marriage guards the eyes and the private parts (Bukhari 5065), which is to say it fences two of the widest doors to sin.

Marriage does not complete a person by magic. It gives the deen a house to live in: shared prayer, shared money handled lawfully, desire given its lane, and somebody who will tell you the truth.

Bukhari 5065
  • The khitba is an intention declared, nothing more: the two remain strangers in law until the ‘aqd.
  • The contract is the door. The feast announces it. The decades after it are the actual marriage.

The road, in order. Four stations, and the gold one is the contract.

Two

Choosing well

الاخْتِيَار

al-ikhtiyar · The tradition is blunt about what to look for, and equally blunt that nobody may be forced.

Sunnah

Marry the deen

A woman is married for four things, he ﷺ said: her wealth, her lineage, her beauty, and her religion. Then the instruction: choose the one with religion, may your hands find dust (Bukhari 5090). The same measure runs the other way; a man's deen and character are the questions to ask before his salary.

Deen here is not a costume. It shows in how a person treats the waiter, the parents, the ex, and the promise made when nobody is watching.

Bukhari 5090
Sunnah

Consent is not decoration

No previously married woman is to be married without her instruction, and no virgin without her permission being sought (Bukhari 5136). A woman came to the Prophet ﷺ saying her father had married her off against her will; he gave her the choice to undo it.

A marriage built on a forced yes is a wrong from its first hour. Families advise, and their counsel is a mercy; the yes itself belongs to the two who will live it.

Bukhari 5136
Sunnah

Look, then decide

When a companion told him ﷺ he intended to marry, he said: look at her, for it is more fitting that love should last between you (Tirmidhi 1087). The meeting is lawful, chaperoned, and purposeful; the tradition never asked anyone to marry a stranger sight unseen.

What the looking is for: not a photoshoot but a conversation with the guard down. Ask real questions. Money, children, parents, where home will be, and what the deen looks like on an ordinary Tuesday.

Tirmidhi 1087
Editorial

Istikhara, and honest counsel

Pray the istikhara and mean it, then act; the answer of istikhara is not a dream or an omen but the way Allah eases or closes the road once you move. Beside it, take counsel from people who know the person, and weigh it without fear.

Two warnings from the counselling room: do not marry a project, hoping to rebuild someone; and do not mistake intensity for compatibility. The quiet questions, money, temper, prayer, in-laws, decide more years than chemistry does.

Three

The contract & the feast

العَقْد

al-‘aqd · An offer, an acceptance, a guardian, witnesses, and a gift. The machinery is small on purpose.

Qur’an

The mahr is hers

Give the women their bridal gift graciously (4:4). The mahr belongs to the bride herself, not to her father and not to the couple's future budget. It may be grand or modest; a companion was married for the teaching of Qur'an he could offer, and Umm Sulaym accepted Abu Talha's Islam as hers.

A heavy mahr is no proof of honour and a light one no proof of cheapness. What the gift proves is that entry into this covenant costs the man something he means.

Qur’an 4:4
Scholars differ

Conditions may be written

The schools differ on which added conditions bind, and how. Some hold a wife may stipulate, for example, not to be moved from her city, and the condition holds; others narrow it. What is agreed: conditions that undo the essence of marriage are void, and promises made at the contract are the most deserving of being kept.

The practical counsel: say the important things out loud before the ‘aqd, write what matters, and take the local scholar through it. Silence at the contract becomes a quarrel in year three.

Sunnah

The walima, and keeping it light

Hold a feast, even with one sheep, he ﷺ told a companion who had married (Bukhari 5167). The marriage is announced and the community eats; that is the whole obligation of a wedding.

It is reported that the marriage with the most blessing is the lightest in cost, and whatever the grading of that wording, its sense is visible in every couple that began under a mountain of wedding debt. Spend on the decades, not the evening.

Bukhari 5167

Four

Living together

العِشْرَة

al-‘ishra · The Qur'an legislates the years, not just the wedding.

Qur’an

Rights in both directions

They have rights like those over them, in fairness (2:228), and: live with them honourably (4:19). Maintenance, kindness, intimacy, counsel, and respect run both ways; neither spouse is staff.

Where duties differ, they differ as functions, not as worth. The house has one qibla and two workloads, and the Qur'an guards each side from the other's worst day.

Qur’an 2:228Qur’an 4:19
Sunnah

The best of you

The best of you are the best to their families, and I am the best of you to my family (Tirmidhi 3895). The Prophet ﷺ mended his own sandals, served his household, raced Aisha and let her win, and never in his life struck a wife or a servant.

Public piety with private harshness fails this hadith completely. The masjid does not launder what happens at home.

Tirmidhi 3895
Sunnah

What stays in the room

Among the worst of people before Allah on the Day of Judgment, he ﷺ said, is the one who goes to his wife and she to him, and then he spreads her secret (Muslim 1437). Intimacy is honoured and it is private; broadcasting it, to friends or to an audience, is a betrayal by name.

The same modesty protects the marriage's quarrels. Solve them inside, or with the arbiters the Qur'an names, not on a feed.

Muslim 1437
Qur’an

Her money is her own

To men a share of what they earn, and to women a share of what they earn (4:32). The wife's income, inheritance and mahr are hers; the household's maintenance is the husband's duty regardless. If she spends on the home, it is charity from her, not a debt she owed.

Money quarrels sink marriages that love could have carried. Put the accounts in the open early: who pays what, what is shared, what is private, and what is saved.

Qur’an 4:32
  • Rights answer rights: they have rights like those over them, in fairness (Qur’an 2:228).
  • The beam rests on mercy, not on winning. A marriage kept by scorekeeping alone tips with every gust.

The scale of the house. Two pans, level, resting on one word.

Five

When it storms

الصُّلْح

as-sulh · The tradition plans for conflict the way an architect plans for wind.

Qur’an

Talk, then bring the arbiters

If you fear a breach between the two, appoint an arbiter from his people and an arbiter from hers; if the two wish to set things right, Allah will bring them together (4:35). The Qur'an's marriage counselling is structural: one wise person from each family, in the room, wanting repair.

Escalation has an order: speak plainly to each other first, involve the arbiters before the lawyers, and keep the children out of the crossfire at every stage.

Qur’an 4:35
Qur’an

Divorce without cruelty

Retain honourably, or release with excellence (2:229). Divorce in Islam is lawful, disliked, and sometimes the most merciful thing left. The Qur'an regulates it in detail precisely because pretending it never happens only makes it crueller when it does.

A woman may also seek the ending, khula, returning the mahr, as the wife of Thabit ibn Qays did before the Prophet ﷺ, without a single word of blame recorded against her (Bukhari 5273).

Qur’an 2:229Bukhari 5273
Editorial

After the storm

The iddah, the waiting period, is not a punishment; it protects lineage, allows tempers to cool, and more than one marriage has been quietly resumed inside it. If the parting stands, the Qur'an's word remains in force: with excellence.

A community that shames divorced men and women contradicts the Book it claims to follow. Among the honoured women of this ummah are divorcees, beginning in the Prophet's ﷺ own household.
