Kit two · the starter guide

الْبِدَايَة

A new house with little means

For eight people with a lease, a key and no idea what comes next. What Allah requires, what the Prophet ﷺ practised, and what is only custom, kept apart and said plainly, with a checklist you can print and carry.

Before you sign anything

النِّيَّة

This guide is written for a small group with a key, a rented room and almost no money. It separates three things that get mixed together: what Allah and His Messenger ﷺ actually require, what centuries of Muslims have found wise, and what is simply custom in one country and not in another. Where something is custom, it says so.

The promise that should carry you

The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever builds a masjid for Allah, Allah builds for him a house like it in Paradise. Sunnah Bukhari 450, Muslim 533

Read that carefully: he said builds a masjid for Allah, not builds a beautiful masjid, not builds a large one. A swept unit above a shop with eight men and their families praying Isha in it is a masjid. The reward is attached to the intention and the deed, not to the marble.

And the Qur'an warns about the opposite intention, a place raised for rivalry or harm rather than for Allah, and praises instead the masjid founded upon taqwa, mindfulness of Allah, from the very first day. Qur'an Qur'an 9:107-108 Decide, before the lease, that this room belongs to Allah and to nobody's name.

What is actually required for a place of prayer

Almost nothing. The Prophet ﷺ said the earth has been made for him a place of prayer and a means of purification, so wherever a Muslim is when the time comes, let him pray. Sunnah Bukhari 335, Muslim 521

A dome, a minaret, a carpet with printed arches, a chandelier, a marble mihrab, a separate entrance, a nameplate: every one of these is custom, and none is a condition. Custom The masjid of the Prophet ﷺ in Madinah was mud brick, palm trunks for pillars and palm fronds for a roof, and rain came through it. Bukhari 446

What you truly need: a clean space you are allowed to use, the direction of the qibla, water for wudu somewhere within reach, and people.

The room itself

الْمَكَان

Choose for the people, not for the photograph.

Choosing it

Ask five questions before the money moves. Can you get out fast if there is a fire, and is there a second way out? Is it on a bus route or near parking, because a masjid nobody can reach is a room nobody prays in? Is the landlord written down as permitting religious use, so that a complaint cannot end you in a month? Is there water, or can water be carried to it? And can you pay the rent for twelve months from money you already have, without borrowing?

If the last answer is no, take a smaller room. A small room you keep is worth more than a hall you lose. Our counsel

Cleaning it

The Prophet ﷺ ordered that masjids be made in the districts where people live, and that they be kept clean and made fragrant. Sunnah Abu Dawud 455, graded sound Ibrahim and Isma'il were commanded to purify the House for those who circle it and those who bow and prostrate. Qur'an Qur'an 2:125

So: scrub the floor before you lay anything on it. Cheap foam mats or a clean donated carpet are enough; carpet with prayer arches printed on it is custom and costs three times as much. Custom Ventilate the room, because a room that smells is a room people leave early. Keep a bin by the door, a mop in the corner, and a rota with real names on it, because "everyone" cleans nothing.

The Prophet ﷺ disliked the smell of raw garlic and onion in the masjid and told those who had eaten them to keep away from the congregation until the smell passed. Sunnah Bukhari 855, Muslim 564 The principle is wider than garlic: do not bring into the room what drives your brother out of it.

The direction

الْقِبْلَة

Allah commanded the turning of the face toward al-Masjid al-Haram, and told the believers that wherever they are, they turn their faces toward it. Qur'an Qur'an 2:144 So the direction is not decoration; it is the one measurement in the room that matters.

Get it right with the qibla tool, which gives the bearing from true north, or with the sun and a stick if no compass in the building can be trusted. Then mark the line on the floor with tape and lay the rows square to it, once, properly. A room set two degrees off for twenty years is a small sadness; a room set forty degrees off because somebody guessed is a real one.

If you genuinely cannot determine it, make your best effort and pray. The Prophet ﷺ said that what is between the east and the west is qibla, for people whose qibla lay to the south. Sunnah Tirmidhi 342, graded sound And Allah says that to Him belong the east and the west, so wherever you turn, there is the face of Allah. Qur'an Qur'an 2:115 Effort is accepted. Laziness pretending to be effort is not.

A mihrab, the niche in the wall, is custom, and a useful one: it tells a stranger where to stand without anyone speaking. A strip of tape and a line of chairs against the qibla wall does the same work for nothing. Custom

The smallest congregation

الْجَمَاعَة

Two people are enough. When Ibn Abbas prayed the night prayer beside the Prophet ﷺ, he stood at his left and the Prophet ﷺ moved him around to his right, which is the arrangement of a congregation of two. Sunnah Bukhari 117, Muslim 763 Some quote a separate report saying "two and above are a congregation"; scholars grade that particular wording weak, so we rest the ruling on the practice above rather than on a shaky text. Named honestly

The prayer in congregation is worth twenty seven times the prayer prayed alone. Sunnah Bukhari 645, Muslim 650 That multiplier does not wait for a building. It is available tomorrow, in your room, with two people.

The imam stands in front, alone, in the middle of the row behind him. One follower stands to the imam's right, level with him. Two or more stand behind. Rows are filled from the front, straightened, and the gaps closed; the Prophet ﷺ used to walk along the rows and straighten them himself. Sunnah Bukhari 717, Muslim 433

One honest minimum: the qibla wall, straight rows filled from the front, shoes off the prayer floor, water near a drain, and a door nothing stands in front of. How the space for women is arranged is custom and varies; what is not optional is that it is clean, equal in quality, and has its own clear way in and out.

The adhan and the iqama

الْأَذَان

The adhan, the call to prayer, came to the community through the dream of Abdullah ibn Zayd, confirmed by the Prophet ﷺ, who told him to teach the words to Bilal because his voice carried further. Sunnah Abu Dawud 499, graded sound

In a new space, the call is normally given inside the room, quietly, for the people who are there. Loudspeakers outside are custom, and in most countries a legal matter; a small community should not spend its first goodwill on a noise complaint. Custom and local law Nothing about the reward of the adhan depends on the volume.

Malik ibn al-Huwayrith and his companions were told by the Prophet ﷺ: when the prayer comes, let one of you give the adhan for you, and let the eldest of you lead you. Sunnah Bukhari 628, Muslim 674 Two young men in a rented room are already inside that instruction.

The iqama, the second and shorter call, is given immediately before the prayer begins, by anyone. A phone playing a recording is not the sunnah being fulfilled; a human voice, however plain, is. Our counsel Teach the words to two people so the prayer never waits for one man's car.

Choosing who leads

الْإِمَامَة

The Prophet ﷺ set the order plainly: the people are led by the one who recites the Book of Allah best; if they are equal in recitation, then the one with most knowledge of the sunnah; if equal in that, then the one who emigrated first; if equal in that, then the eldest. Sunnah Muslim 673

Notice what is not in the list: wealth, the size of the donation, the passport, the family name, or who signed the lease. A community that lets the biggest donor lead has sold something it cannot buy back. Our counsel

A paid imam is custom and often necessary later; it is not a condition for a masjid to exist. Custom Until you can pay someone properly, rotate among those qualified, write the rota on the wall, and let no one carry it alone. And when the imam is also the plumber and the treasurer, that is a warning sign about the community, not about him.

Keep one rule from the first month: whoever leads is answerable to the community, and the community is answerable to Allah for choosing him. Disagreements about leadership have emptied more prayer rooms than rent ever has.

The first jumu'ah

الْجُمُعَة

Allah commands that when the call is made on the day of jumu'ah, the Friday gathering, you hurry to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trade. Qur'an Qur'an 62:9

What it requires

Its time is the time of dhuhr, the midday prayer. It is two units of prayer led aloud, preceded by two khutbas, sermons, delivered standing with a short sitting between them. Sunnah Bukhari 928, Muslim 861 It is prayed in congregation, in a settled place, not by a traveller in passing.

What is genuinely disputed

The minimum number of people. The schools differ sharply: some require forty, some twelve, some three besides the imam, and some accept any group in which congregation is possible. Scholars differ We will not pretend this is settled. What we can say honestly is that the second jumu'ah in the history of Islam was prayed in a small village masjid at Jawatha in Bahrain, far from Madinah and far from a crowd. Bukhari 892

So do this: ask a scholar your community already trusts which position it follows, follow that, and write it down. If your school requires forty and you are nine, you pray dhuhr instead, without shame and without argument, until Allah sends the fortieth.

The khutba itself

Short. The Prophet ﷺ taught that a long prayer and a short sermon are a sign of a man's understanding of the religion. Sunnah Muslim 869 The khutba builder holds you to that with a clock, gives you the opening in Arabic with its translation, and prints in type large enough to read while standing.

A minbar, the raised pulpit, is custom. A chair, or standing on the floor, is fine. Custom

Keeping the times

الْمَوَاقِيت

The prayer is prescribed upon the believers at fixed times. Qur'an Qur'an 4:103 A new community earns trust fastest by starting on time, every time, whether four people came or forty.

Print the month from the timetable tool and tape it by the door: paper needs no power and survives every outage. Verify one day against a timetable your city already trusts before you print thirty of them, and put the iqama times, the times you actually begin, next to the adhan times, because those are the ones people plan their day around.

Announce a change before you make it, not after. Nothing breaks a small congregation faster than arriving to a locked door at the time printed on the sheet.

Women and children, welcomed properly

أَهْلُ الْبَيْت

The Prophet ﷺ said: do not prevent the female servants of Allah from the masjids of Allah. Sunnah Bukhari 900, Muslim 442 This is a prohibition addressed to men, and a new masjid that quietly ignores it has failed in its first month.

Practically, and cheaply: give women a clean, lit, ventilated part of the room, not a cupboard, not a basement with a broken speaker, and not the space left over. Make sure they can hear the imam and reach the door without walking through the men's rows. Whether the space is a section of the same room, a curtained area or a separate room is custom and varies by community and country; what is required is that they are not prevented, and what is decent is that the quality is equal. Custom

About children: the Prophet ﷺ would shorten a prayer he had intended to lengthen because he heard a child crying, out of mercy for the mother. Sunnah Bukhari 707, Muslim 470 He also prayed while carrying his granddaughter Umama, putting her down when he prostrated and lifting her when he stood. Bukhari 516, Muslim 543

So the noise of a child is not a fitna to be scolded out of the room. Set a corner with a box of quiet toys, tell the men in the first month that children are expected here, and let the one who came with a toddler leave with her dignity.

Wudu and cleanliness where there is almost no plumbing

الطَّهَارَة

You do not need a wudu room with twelve taps. The Prophet ﷺ used to make wudu with a mudd of water, roughly two thirds of a litre, and wash from janaba with a saa, roughly two and a half litres. Sunnah Bukhari 201, Muslim 325 Waste is the enemy, not scarcity.

Three cheap arrangements that work: a kettle or a jug filled at the kitchen tap and poured over a basin; a plastic tub with a drain hose into a bucket that somebody empties; a low tap over a tray with a bench beside it so nobody has to balance on one leg. Put a mat and a towel there. Mop it after every prayer, because a wet floor in a rented unit is how a lease is lost.

Two mercies that save water and dignity: wiping over leather socks or khuff instead of washing the feet, which the Prophet ﷺ did and which Mughira ibn Shu'ba reported, Bukhari 206, Muslim 274 and tayammum, purification with clean earth, when there is genuinely no water or using it would harm you. Qur'an Qur'an 5:6 The details of both have conditions; learn them from a teacher rather than from a guess.

Keep the room itself clean the way you would keep a guest room: shoes off at the edge, a rack or a shelf so the pile does not spread, bins emptied weekly, and no food left overnight. Mice arrive faster than donors.

Keeping the space open and safe

الْأَمَانَة

The masjids belong to Allah, so call upon none besides Him in them. Qur'an Qur'an 72:18 A place that belongs to Allah cannot become one family's front room, one group's political office, or one man's platform.

Write six lines and pin them inside the door in the first month. Our counsel, not revelation

Who holds keys, by name, and who they answer to. The opening hours, and what happens if the key holder is ill. That no adult is ever alone with a child who is not their own, and that classes happen with two adults present or the door open. Where the fire exits are, and that nothing is ever stored in front of them. That no fundraising, no speaker and no political campaign uses this room without the trustees agreeing in writing. And a plain sentence that anyone may ask to see the accounts.

Check the boring things: a working smoke alarm, a fire extinguisher, a first aid box, the landlord's insurance and whether you need your own, and an electrical inspection if the wiring is old. None of this is glamorous, and all of it is part of the trust you accepted when you took the keys.

The money, transparent from the first day

الْمَال

This is where small masjids die, and it is entirely preventable. The Prophet ﷺ appointed a man to collect zakat, and when the man returned saying "this is for you and this was given to me as a gift", the Prophet ﷺ stood on the minbar and asked why he had not sat in his father's house to see whether gifts came to him there. Sunnah Bukhari 7174, Muslim 1832 Public money is accounted for in public, and the messenger of Allah ﷺ did that accounting from the pulpit.

The four rules, from day one

One. Nobody handles cash alone. Two named people count it, both sign, on the same day.

Two. Every coin is written down as it arrives, in one book or one shared spreadsheet, with the date and what it was given for. A donation box that is emptied without a record is an invitation to suspicion, and suspicion has closed more prayer rooms than poverty.

Three. Every payment out has a receipt, and no payment goes to a trustee, a trustee's business or a trustee's family without the others agreeing in writing and the community being told.

Four. The total in, the total out and the balance are read aloud once a month after a prayer, and the book is open to anyone who asks, without them having to explain why they asked.

Keep zakat money in a separate line from general donations. Zakat has eight named recipients in the Qur'an. Qur'an Qur'an 9:60 The majority of scholars hold that zakat may not be spent on building or rent; a minority of later scholars permit some of it under "in the path of Allah". Scholars differ Ask the scholar your community follows, decide once, write the decision in the book, and tell the donors which fund their money entered.

Four steps and one loop. Nothing here needs software, a charity number or an accountant: it needs one notebook, two honest people and the willingness to read the numbers out loud.

Growing without debt

النَّمَاء

Allah has permitted trade and forbidden riba, interest, and the warning that follows it in the Qur'an is the sharpest in the book on any financial matter. Qur'an Qur'an 2:275-279 An interest-bearing mortgage taken to buy a building for the worship of Allah is a contradiction that no amount of good intention repairs. Whatever your fiqh position on necessity, a masjid is not a starving man.

Even lawful debt is heavy: the Prophet ﷺ said the soul of the believer remains attached to his debt until it is paid. Sunnah Tirmidhi 1078, graded sound A community that borrows to grow can spend a decade serving a lender instead of its neighbours.

How to grow instead

Grow in this order: rent, then a longer lease, then a bigger rent, and only then buy, with money already gathered. When the Prophet ﷺ came to Madinah and chose the ground for the masjid, its owners, two orphan boys, offered it as a gift; he refused to take it without payment and paid its price. Sunnah Bukhari 428 Clean title, clean money, no obligation hanging over the house.

Put the property in the name of a trust or a registered charity, never in the name of a person, however trusted. People die, marry, divorce and fall out; a masjid registered to an individual has been lost more than once. Our counsel

And remember the arithmetic Allah taught: charity does not decrease wealth. Sunnah Muslim 2588 A congregation that sees an honest book gives more than one that is asked loudly and told nothing.

A last word

You will be tired, the heating will fail in the coldest week, someone will argue about the carpet, and one Friday four people will come. Keep opening the door. The house of Allah is not the building; it is the opening of the door at the right time, again and again, until Allah sends more people than the room can hold, and you begin looking for a bigger room with the same clean book and the same honest intention.
