الحَجّ

The journey of a lifetime, explained from the beginning

Al-Hajj, the pilgrimage, is the fifth pillar of Islam and the one journey a Muslim makes once in a life if he is able. This room is written for someone who has never gone: what you do, on which day, in what order, what is a pillar and what is only better, where the schools of law differ, and what to do when something goes wrong.

What this room is, and what it is not

This is a guide to understand and to prepare. It is not a fatwa, and it cannot be one. A fatwa is a ruling given to a named person in a known situation by a scholar who can ask questions back. Nothing on this page knows your school, your health, your group, or what happened to you at two in the morning at the miqat, the boundary where ihram begins.

Rulings on the ground also change. Routes are moved, buses are re-timed, whole camps are shifted, and the way your group performs a rite may be shaped by the crowd around you and by decisions taken that week. Follow your group’s scholar and the guide walking with you. They can see what a page cannot.

Where the four Sunni schools differ, this room says so and gives the positions fairly, because a reader who knows there is a difference asks a question, and a reader who was told there is only one answer argues with the person beside him. Where a report is beloved but its chain is contested, that is said plainly rather than borrowed quietly. Where a number for a narration is not certain, the narration is named by its collection and its wording instead of being guessed at.

About the hadith on this page. Most are cited by their collection and by the wording of the narration rather than by number, because the numbering differs from one printing to another and a number is an easy thing to copy wrongly. Where a number is given, it is one we are confident of. Look the narration up either way, and confirm every ruling here with the scholars of your own group before you act on it.

Read it slowly, more than once, before you travel. Then take it with you, and ask a scholar on site the moment anything is unclear. Asking is not weakness. It is the whole method.

الحَجّ

What Hajj is, and who it is written upon

al-Hajj, the pilgrimage

Before the map and the days and the rulings, the shape of the thing. Hajj is a set of acts done in one place, in a fixed handful of days, once in a life, by whoever can reach it. It is worship with your legs. It is also the oldest invitation still open: a call Ibrahim was told to make, that people are still answering.

Qur’an 22:27. And proclaim to the people the pilgrimage: they will come to you on foot and on every lean camel, from every distant mountain pass. One man called out in a valley with nobody in it. Every crowd since has been the echo, and the first word you say when you enter ihram is not a request but a reply.

The fifth pillar, and its verse

Qur’an

Islam is built on five things, and the fifth is Hajj to the House for whoever can find a way to it. The Qur’an states the duty in one sentence: to Allah, from the people, is a pilgrimage to the House, for whoever is able to find a way there. The verse does not say every year, and it does not say everyone. It says whoever is able, once.

The Prophet ﷺ said it plainly when a man asked whether Hajj was owed every year: had he said yes, it would have become binding every year, and Hajj is once, and whatever a person does beyond that is voluntary. So one Hajj discharges the duty of a lifetime. Everything after that is a gift you give, not a debt you pay.

What able actually means

Scholars differ

The Qur’an’s condition is istita’ah, ability, and the scholars unpack it into four plain things. Your body can bear the journey. You have the money for the travel and the rites. That money is beyond what your dependents need while you are away, so nobody at home goes hungry so that you can stand at Arafah. And the road is safe enough to travel with your life and your property intact.

A report explaining the way as provision and a mount is narrated by Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah. Scholars have criticised its chains, and it is honest to say so, but the four schools agree on its meaning, and they built the same four conditions from the verse itself.

Debt is where most people’s question actually lives. The rule the schools work with is that a debt owed now, that a person could pay now, comes before a voluntary journey and before an obligation he is not yet able for. A long term debt like a mortgage that is being paid on schedule is treated differently by many scholars from a debt already due. This is exactly the kind of question to put to a scholar with your actual numbers in front of him.

Where the schools part

The four schools

agree on the four conditions of ability: body, wealth beyond dependants’ needs, means of travel, and a safe road.

Hanafi and Maliki

hold that a person unable to travel because of permanent illness is not obliged in himself, though the Hanafis hold that if he had wealth and left instruction for a Hajj to be done for him, that stands.

Shafi’i and Hanbali

hold that the one who is permanently unable in body but able in wealth must have someone perform Hajj on his behalf, on the strength of the hadith of the woman of Khath’am.

Hajj for someone who cannot go

Sunnah

A woman of the tribe of Khath’am asked the Prophet ﷺ about her father, who was old and could not sit on a mount. He told her to perform Hajj on his behalf. That is the anchor for badal al-hajj, Hajj performed on behalf of another who is permanently unable, or who has died.

One condition is well known and worth saying: the one performing it should have performed his own Hajj first. The Prophet ﷺ heard a man saying labbayk on behalf of Shubrumah, asked who Shubrumah was, and told him to perform Hajj for himself first and then for Shubrumah. The Hanafis differ, holding the Hajj still counts for the one it was intended for while the performer is blameworthy, but the safe road is the plain one.

Umrah, and how the schools rank it

Scholars differ

Al-Umrah, the lesser pilgrimage, is the same visit without the days. Ihram, the sacred state you enter at a fixed boundary; tawaf, circling the House seven times; sa’i, going seven times between two small hills; and then cutting the hair, and it is done. It has no fixed date: it can be performed at any time of the year, and it takes a few hours rather than five days.

Whether Umrah is obligatory once in a life is one of the oldest live disagreements in this chapter, and it is not a small one. Those who say it is obligatory read the command to complete the Hajj and the Umrah for Allah, and a narration in which the Prophet ﷺ told Aisha that the jihad of women is Hajj and Umrah. Those who say it is a confirmed sunnah point out that the famous hadith of Jibril, which listed Islam’s obligations, named Hajj and did not name Umrah.

Where the schools part

Shafi’i and Hanbali

Umrah is obligatory once in a lifetime for whoever is able.

Hanafi and Maliki

Umrah is sunnah mu’akkadah, a confirmed sunnah, strongly encouraged and not a debt.

A call still being answered

Qur’an

The Qur’an tells it as a scene. Allah settled Ibrahim at the place of the House and told him to purify it for those who circle it, and then to proclaim the pilgrimage to the people, and they will come to you on foot and on every lean camel, from every distant mountain pass. One man called out in an empty valley. Every crowd since has been the echo.

That is why the first thing you say when you enter ihram is not a request but an answer. Labbayk. Here I am. The word is a reply to a call, and the call is older than every person in the crowd around you.

The Qur’an also names what the journey is for, in a phrase easy to walk past: that they may witness benefits for themselves and mention the name of Allah on known days. Benefits, plural, and unspecified. Some of them arrive at Arafah. Some of them do not arrive for years.

What is promised to the one who goes

Sunnah

Two narrations carry the whole weight of the reward. Whoever performs Hajj for Allah and does not speak obscenely nor act corruptly returns as the day his mother bore him. And: the accepted Hajj, al-Hajj al-mabrur, has no reward except Paradise.

Notice the condition sitting inside the first one. It is not the walking that returns a person clean, it is the walking without the obscenity and the quarrelling. The Qur’an says the same in the same breath as it opens the season: no obscenity, no corruption, no arguing in the Hajj.

أنواع النُّسُك

The three ways to perform Hajj

anwa’ an-nusuk, the kinds of rite

There is not one Hajj, there are three shapes of it, and you choose your shape before you enter ihram, not after. The choice decides whether you owe a sacrifice, whether you get out of ihram in the middle, and how many times you perform tawaf and sa’i. Nearly every group from outside Arabia performs the first one.

  • A filled circle is where ihram is entered. An open circle is where it is released.
  • The choice is made before you enter ihram at the miqat, not afterwards, because the sacrifice, the hair cutting and the second sa’i all follow from it.

Qur’an 2:196. A solid bar is time spent in ihram. Only tamattu’ has a gap in it, and that gap is the whole reason most pilgrims from outside the Haram choose it: you cannot stay wrapped in two cloths for the fortnight your flight gives you. The diamond is the hady, the sacrifice, which falls on tamattu’ and qiran and not on ifrad.

Tamattu’: Umrah first, then out, then Hajj

Most commonQur’an

At-Tamattu’ means the enjoyment, and the name is literal. You enter ihram for Umrah at the miqat, perform the Umrah, cut your hair, and come completely out of ihram. You wear ordinary clothes, you go back to your family, everything lawful is lawful again. Then on the eighth of Dhul Hijjah you enter ihram again, from wherever you are staying in Makkah, for Hajj.

It is the Qur’an that names this way and attaches the sacrifice to it: whoever enjoys the Umrah until the Hajj, then whatever sacrifice is easy. Whoever cannot find one fasts three days during the Hajj and seven when he returns, ten complete. The verse then adds the limit: that is for whoever’s family is not present at the Sacred Mosque.

This is what most pilgrims from outside the Haram, the sanctuary around Makkah, do, and there are two honest reasons. Flights arrive days or weeks before the eighth, and nobody can stay in ihram for two weeks without hardship. And the Prophet ﷺ, at the end of his life, ordered the companions who had not brought sacrificial animals to turn their Hajj into an Umrah and come out of ihram, and said that had he known earlier what he knew then, he would not have brought the animal.

Qiran: both together, in one ihram

Sunnah

Al-Qiran means the joining. You make one intention for Umrah and Hajj together at the miqat, and you do not come out of ihram at all between them. You stay in the two cloths from the miqat until the tenth of Dhul Hijjah. A sacrifice is due, as it is for tamattu’.

This is the way of one who brings a sacrificial animal with him, and it is what the Prophet ﷺ himself did in the Farewell Hajj according to the strongest reading of the reports, because he had driven the animals from Madinah.

The schools differ on the mechanics, and the difference is practical. Most schools hold that one tawaf and one sa’i serve for both the Umrah and the Hajj of the qarin. The Hanafis hold that he performs two tawafs and two sa’is, one set for each.

Where the schools part

Hanafi

the qarin performs two tawafs and two sa’is, one for the Umrah and one for the Hajj.

Maliki, Shafi’i and Hanbali

one tawaf and one sa’i suffice for both, on the strength of Aisha’s report that those who joined the two performed one tawaf.

Muslim 1218the Prophet ﷺ drove the sacrificial animals and did not come out of ihramBukhariAisha: those who joined Hajj and Umrah performed one tawaf

Ifrad: Hajj alone

Sunnah

Al-Ifrad means the singling out. You enter ihram for Hajj only, perform the Hajj, and no sacrifice is due from you, because you have not joined an Umrah to it. If you wish to perform an Umrah afterwards, you do it after the Hajj is complete, entering ihram again from outside the sanctuary boundary.

This is the usual choice of the people of Makkah and those living inside the miqats, and of anyone who arrives close to the eighth. It is also the only one of the three with no hady, no sacrifice, attached to it, which is why it is sometimes chosen for reasons of cost. That is a real reason, and it is worth saying out loud that saving the cost of a sheep is not usually the best reason to choose a rite.

Which is best, and why the schools split

Scholars differ

This is a famous four way disagreement, and every school has an argument that is not silly. Do not let anyone tell you their answer is the only one, and do not carry the argument into the camp with you.

Whichever you choose, choose it before you enter ihram, and tell your group leader, because the sacrifice, the timing of your hair cutting, and whether you repeat the sa’i all follow from it. Changing your mind afterwards is possible in some cases and costly in others.

Where the schools part

Hanafi

Qiran is best, because it joins two rites in one journey and the Prophet ﷺ performed it.

Maliki and Shafi’i

Ifrad is best, reading the Prophet’s ﷺ own Hajj as ifrad and noting that no sacrifice is imposed by it.

Hanbali

Tamattu’ is best, on the strength of his ﷺ final instruction to his companions to release into Umrah, and his wish that he had done so himself.

The sacrifice is a thanksgiving, not a fine

Scholars differ

The hady owed by the pilgrim of tamattu’ and qiran is called by the scholars a dam of shukr, a sacrifice of gratitude. It is not a penalty for a mistake. You are not being fined for resting between the two rites. You are giving thanks for having been given two acts of worship in one journey.

Whoever cannot find or afford one fasts three days during the Hajj and seven after returning home. The schools differ on when the three may be fasted: many allow them from the entry into ihram for Umrah, most prefer the seventh, eighth and ninth, and if they are missed the Hanafis and others discuss whether the days of Tashriq, the three days that follow the sacrifice, may be used. If you expect to fast rather than slaughter, settle this with a scholar before you travel, not on the ninth.

Where the schools part

Hanafi

the three days may be fasted after entering ihram for Umrah and before the day of Arafah. That they may not be delayed past the day of Arafah is the well known view, and it is stated here more crisply than the madhhab holds it: Hanafi authorities differ among themselves over what is owed by one who lets those days pass, and they do not all answer the same way.

Maliki and Shafi’i

the standard summary is that they may be fasted from the entry into ihram for Hajj, and that the Shafi’is do not permit them in the days of Tashriq in the strongest view. The Maliki half of that line is thin, and their books are reported with more than one starting point, so a Maliki should settle it with a scholar of his own school.

Hanbali

permits fasting them in the days of Tashriq for one who did not fast them before, on the strength of the report of Aisha and Ibn Umar.

الإحرام

Ihram: the state, the boundary, and the prohibitions

al-ihram, entering the sacred state

Ihram is the most misunderstood word in this whole chapter. It is not the towels. It is a state you enter with an intention at a fixed boundary, and the cloth is only its uniform. Once you are in it, a list of ordinary lawful things becomes unlawful to you until you are released, and some of them cost you a sacrifice if you break them.

  • An aircraft crosses the line in the air. Enter ihram before you pass over it, which in practice means changing at your departure airport and making the intention when the crew announces the miqat.
  • Cross it without ihram while intending the rites and you return to it, or a sacrifice is due.
  • Whoever already lives inside the ring enters ihram from where he is, and goes out to at-Tan’im or al-Ji’ranah only for an Umrah.

Bukhari 1524 and Muslim 1181. He ﷺ appointed them for the people of each direction, and for whoever passes through them from anywhere else, for whoever intends Hajj or Umrah. On the reading of the hadith scholars, four are named in explicit prophetic text, while Dhat Irq is reported in Bukhari as appointed by Umar when the cities of Iraq were opened, with reports raising it to the Prophet ﷺ that those scholars discuss and do not all grade the same way. That assessment is theirs, repeated here. In practice all four schools treat it as the miqat of that direction.

The five miqats, and who enters at each

ObligationSunnah

A miqat is a boundary line in space. The Prophet ﷺ appointed one for the people of each direction, and no person intending Hajj or Umrah may cross his line without being in ihram.

Dhul Hulayfah, for the people of Madinah and anyone coming through it, about four hundred and fifty kilometres out and the furthest of them, known today as Abyar Ali. Al-Juhfah, for the people of Sham, Egypt and the west, north of Makkah, with Rabigh used in its place today. Qarn al-Manazil, for the people of Najd, to the east, known today as as-Sayl al-Kabir. Yalamlam, for the people of Yemen, to the south, used also by many arriving from South and Southeast Asia. And Dhat Irq, for the people of Iraq, to the north east.

The rule he ﷺ gave with them covers everyone: they are for the people of those places, and for anyone who comes through them from other lands, for whoever intends Hajj or Umrah. Whoever lives nearer than a miqat enters ihram from where he is, until the people of Makkah enter from Makkah itself.

One honest note about the fifth, and it is a claim about grading rather than about the rite itself. On the reading of the hadith scholars, four of these are named in explicit prophetic text in Bukhari and Muslim, while Dhat Irq is reported in Bukhari as appointed by Umar for the people of Iraq when their cities were opened, with further reports raising it to the Prophet ﷺ whose chains those scholars discuss and do not all grade the same way. That assessment is theirs and we are repeating it, not making it. In practice all four schools treat Dhat Irq as the miqat of that direction.

Arriving by air, and living inside the line

ObligationScholars differ

An aircraft crosses the miqat at altitude, which the Prophet ﷺ did not describe, so the scholars applied the rule to the vertical. The overwhelming position of contemporary councils and the four schools’ own reasoning is that the boundary extends upward: you enter ihram before your aircraft passes over the line of your miqat, not after you land.

In practice this means changing into the ihram garments at home or at the airport you depart from, and making the intention and beginning the talbiyah, the pilgrim’s call, when the crew announces the miqat or roughly half an hour before landing, whichever comes first. Making the intention early is safe. Making it late is not.

Whoever crosses his miqat without ihram and then intends Hajj or Umrah must go back to the line and enter from there. If he does not go back, the four schools hold a dam, a sacrifice, is due from him, and his Hajj is still valid. Note the word intending: someone travelling to Jeddah for work, with no intention of Umrah, is not bound by the line at the moment he crosses it.

Someone already living inside the miqats, in Jeddah or Makkah, enters ihram for Hajj from where he is. For Umrah he must first go out to the boundary of the sanctuary and enter from there, which is why pilgrims are taken to at-Tan’im or al-Ji’ranah. That practice comes from the Prophet ﷺ sending Aisha out with her brother to enter ihram for Umrah from at-Tan’im.

Where the schools part

The four schools

agree that crossing the miqat without ihram while intending the rites obliges either a return to the line or a sacrifice.

Hanafi

hold that if he returns to the miqat and enters ihram there, the sacrifice falls away; the Shafi’is hold similarly, and the difference between them lies in whether the return must be before he begins any rite.

What ihram actually is

PillarScholars differ

Ihram is an intention, made in the heart, to enter the rites. The cloth is not the ihram. A man wrapped in two white sheets who has not intended anything is a man in two sheets. A man in ordinary clothes who intends Hajj at the miqat has entered ihram and is now sinning by wearing the clothes, not waiting outside the state.

The bodily preparations are sunnah, and they are these: ghusl, a full washing, which is recommended even for a woman who is menstruating or in post natal bleeding, because Asma bint Umays gave birth at Dhul Hulayfah and the Prophet ﷺ told her to wash, bind herself, and enter ihram. Then perfume on the body, not on the garments, which Aisha said she used to apply to him ﷺ before he entered ihram. Then, for a man, the two unstitched white cloths: an izar wrapped around the lower body and a rida’ over the shoulders. Then two units of prayer if it is a permitted time, then the intention as the journey begins, then the talbiyah.

For a woman there is no special garment at all. Her ihram is her ordinary modest dress, in any colour, and this needs saying because so many people assume white is required of her. What changes for her is that she does not wear the niqab, a face veil cut to the face, and does not wear gloves, because the Prophet ﷺ named those two by name.

The schools differ on whether the talbiyah is part of entering the state or a sunnah alongside it, which matters if someone forgets to say it.

Where the schools part

Hanafi

the intention alone does not convene ihram; it must be joined to the talbiyah or to driving the sacrificial animal.

Maliki

the talbiyah is wajib, an obligation, and a sacrifice is due from one who omits it entirely.

Shafi’i and Hanbali

the intention convenes the ihram, and the talbiyah is a strong sunnah alongside it.

The talbiyah, the words of the answer

SunnahSunnah

This is what Ibn Umar reported as the talbiyah of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and it is the sound of the whole journey. Men raise their voices with it. Women say it audibly to themselves without raising the voice.

Say it often: when you climb, when you descend, when you meet a group of people, after every prayer, in the morning, whenever it comes to you. It stops at different points depending on your rite: for Umrah, when you begin the tawaf; for Hajj, when you throw the first pebble at Jamrat al-Aqabah, the largest of the three stone pillars at Mina, on the tenth.

The prohibitions that carry an expiation

Qur’an

These are the ones with a price attached. Removing hair from the head or body, and by agreement the cutting of nails. Applying perfume to the body or clothes, including scented soap, scented cream and scented deodorant. For a man, wearing anything stitched or shaped to a limb: shirt, trousers, underwear, socks, and covering the head with anything resting on it. For a woman, the niqab and gloves. Touching a spouse with desire. Hunting land game, or helping someone else hunt it.

The expiation for the hair, and by extension for the others in this group, is set in the Qur’an and explained by the Prophet ﷺ to Ka’b ibn Ujrah, whose head was covered in lice: fast three days, or feed six poor people, or slaughter a sheep. The one who does it out of a genuine need, an illness or an injury, pays the same expiation without sin. The one who does it deliberately without need pays it and repents.

Hunting has its own expiation, named in the Qur’an: a like animal from the livestock, judged by two just men, or the feeding of the poor, or an equivalent in fasting. Sea game stays lawful throughout. And note the boundary: buying meat that someone else lawfully slaughtered is not hunting.

What breaks the Hajj outright, and what only wounds it

Scholars differ

There is one prohibition in a class of its own. Sexual intercourse before the first release on the tenth invalidates the Hajj itself, by the agreement of the four schools. The pilgrim must complete the rites anyway, must repeat the Hajj the following year, and owes a badanah, a camel. It is worth being honest that this ruling is built on the judgements of the Companions, chiefly Umar, Ali and Ibn Abbas, rather than on an explicit prophetic text, and that the schools accepted it as settled.

Intercourse after the first release but before tawaf al-ifadah is different: the Hajj is not invalidated, an expiation is due, and one re-enters ihram from outside the sanctuary to complete the remaining tawaf. The details differ by school and this is a scholar’s question.

At the other end of the list sit the prohibitions with no material expiation at all: arguing, quarrelling, obscene speech and sin, named in the Qur’an as the things that hollow out a Hajj from inside. There is no sheep for these. There is repentance, and there is the person you were rude to, whose forgiveness you now have to go and ask for.

The famous differences inside the list

Scholars differ

Three of these come up on nearly every journey, and knowing that the schools differ will save you an argument in a tent.

First, the marriage contract. The Prophet ﷺ said the one in ihram may not marry, nor give another in marriage, nor propose. Most scholars therefore hold such a contract void with no expiation. The Hanafis hold it valid though disliked, reading the report that he ﷺ married Maymunah while in ihram.

Second, footwear. Ibn Umar reported the prohibition of the khuff, a leather sock covering the ankle, unless a man finds no sandals, in which case he cuts them below the ankles. But in the sermon at Arafah, as Ibn Abbas reports, he ﷺ said that whoever finds no sandals may wear boots, without mentioning cutting. Most schools require the cutting; the Hanbalis, and some others, take the later Arafah wording and do not.

Third, and most useful of all: what happens if you break a prohibition by accident, out of forgetfulness or not knowing. This is not a small technicality. It happens to almost everyone, usually with scented soap.

Where the schools part

Hanafi and Maliki

the standard summary is that the expiation is due even from one who forgot or did not know, because it repairs a right of the rite rather than punishing a sin. That summary flattens the Maliki position, which carries recognised exceptions and excuses in a number of these cases, so a Maliki should not read this line as the whole of his school.

Shafi’i and Hanbali

excuse the forgetful and the genuinely ignorant in several of these, notably perfume, clothing and covering the head, holding no expiation is due once the thing is removed at once.

All four

agree that the moment you realise, you stop, you remove it, and you do not carry on.

العُمْرَة

Umrah, step by step

al-Umrah, the lesser pilgrimage

Umrah is four steps and a few hours, and it is the best possible rehearsal for Hajj, because tawaf and sa’i return in the middle of the fifth day when you are tired. Learn them properly here, once, calmly, and the tenth of Dhul Hijjah will be far easier.

  • Kiss the Stone if there is space. If not, face it, raise your right hand, say Allahu akbar, and keep walking. A sunnah never sits on top of harming someone.
  • The Hijr, the low curved wall, is part of the House. Your circuit goes outside it, not through it.
  • Ramal, the brisk short step in the first three circuits, and idtiba’, the bared right shoulder, are for men only, and both drop away in a crowd.

Three: tawaf, seven circuits

PillarQur’an

Tawaf is circling the Kaaba seven times, anticlockwise, with the House on your left. It starts at the corner of al-Hajar al-Aswad, the Black Stone, and it ends there. Every circuit is one full pass from that corner back to it. Counting matters more than speed; lose count and you build on the lower number you are certain of.

The conditions are: covering what must be covered, being inside the mosque, keeping the House on your left, staying outside the Hijr, that low curved wall, because it is part of the House itself as Aisha was told, and beginning and ending at the Stone. Purity is the one condition the schools split on, and it matters enormously for women, so it has its own note below.

At the Stone, do istilam, the greeting of the Stone: kiss it if you can reach it without harming anyone, or touch it with your hand and kiss your hand, or, and this is what almost everyone will do, face it, raise your right hand toward it and say Allahu akbar, and keep walking. At the Yamani corner, touch it with the right hand if you can, and do not kiss it and do not point at it if you cannot. Between the Yamani corner and the Black Stone, say the words the Prophet ﷺ said there, which are a verse: our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire.

For men only, in a tawaf that will be followed by sa’i: ramal, walking briskly with short quick steps, in the first three circuits, and idtiba’, passing the upper cloth under the right armpit so the right shoulder is bare, for the whole tawaf. Both are sunnah, both drop away in a crowd where they would harm someone, and neither applies to women.

Nothing else in the tawaf has fixed words. There is no prescribed du’a for the first circuit or the fourth. Say what you want, recite Qur’an, remember Allah, ask for your mother.

أيّام الحَجّ

Hajj, day by day

ayyam al-Hajj, the days of the pilgrimage

Everything above was preparation. This is the Hajj itself: six dates, four places, and an order that does not move. Read the map first, then the days. If you learn nothing else on this page, learn where you are meant to be on the ninth, and why the whole thing turns on it.

  • 8 Dhul Hijjah. Ihram again for tamattu’, out to Mina, five prayers there, shortened and not joined.
  • 9 Dhul Hijjah. To Arafah after sunrise. Zuhr and Asr joined. Stand until sunset. Then to Muzdalifah, where Maghrib and Isha are prayed on arrival.
  • 10 Dhul Hijjah. Back to Mina at dawn: seven pebbles at the large pillar, the sacrifice, the hair, then down to the House for tawaf al-ifadah.
  • 11, 12 and 13. At Mina, stoning all three pillars each afternoon. Leave after the twelfth if you hurry, then the farewell tawaf, and home.

The single most useful picture in this room. Four places, six legs, six dates. Learn this shape before you fly and the days stop being a blur of buses. Everything on this map can be repaired with a sacrifice or performed late, except the leg to Arafah on the ninth. Miss that one and there is no Hajj this year.

Qur’an 2:203. Whoever hurries in two days, there is no sin upon him, and whoever delays, there is no sin upon him. The verse says it twice, in both directions, so neither choice is a compromise. What is not optional is the ninth: the Prophet ﷺ said al-Hajju Arafah, Hajj is Arafah.

Four: tawaf al-ifadah, and its sa’i

PillarQur’an

Pillar. Leave this out and there is no Hajj at all, and no sacrifice replaces it.

Go down to Makkah and perform tawaf al-ifadah, seven circuits, exactly as before. This is the second pillar of the Hajj and the one every school agrees on. Without it there is no Hajj, no matter what else was done, and no sacrifice replaces it. It is also called tawaf az-ziyarah, the tawaf of the visit.

The pilgrim of tamattu’ then performs sa’i again, because his earlier sa’i belonged to his Umrah. The pilgrim of ifrad or qiran who already performed sa’i after his arrival tawaf does not repeat it, in the view of the majority; the Hanafis have the qarin perform a second sa’i.

With this tawaf, and its sa’i where it is owed, comes at-tahallul ath-thani, the second release. Now everything is lawful, including your spouse. Then return to Mina for the night.

On timing: the schools agree the day of sacrifice is best. If you cannot manage it today, in the crowds and the heat, know that the Hanafis hold it must be done within the days of sacrifice or a sacrifice becomes due, while the majority hold there is no final deadline and delay is merely disliked. Many groups deliberately send pilgrims for this tawaf on the eleventh or twelfth night, and that is a considered choice, not negligence.

The order, and what if you get it wrong

Scholars differ

The sunnah order of the four is: stone, slaughter, shave, then tawaf. That is what he ﷺ did, and it is what you should aim for.

But the Prophet ﷺ spent that day at Mina being asked about mistakes, and answered every one the same way. A man said: I shaved before I slaughtered. He said: do it, no harm. What invalidates, what costs a sacrifice, and what is merely better

al-arkan wa al-wajibat, the pillars and the obligations

Every act of the Hajj sits in one of three ranks, and the rank tells you what happens if it is missed. A rukn, a pillar, cannot be replaced by anything: leave it and there is no Hajj. A wajib, an obligation, is repaired by a dam, a sacrifice, and the Hajj stands. A sunnah is better and costs nothing. The schools do not put every act in the same rank, and the two tables below say plainly where they part.

The whole law of repair, in three lines. A dam is a sacrifice, a sheep or a goat or a seventh of a camel, slaughtered inside the sanctuary and given to its poor. It is not a fine and nobody profits from it. Only one act in the entire Hajj sits in the top band with no way back at all, and that is the standing at Arafah.

The three ranks, in one breath

Editorial

Rukn, pillar: the act is part of the thing itself. If you omit it, you have not performed the Hajj, and you remain in ihram until you perform it or the Hajj is written off and made up. Money cannot buy it back.

Wajib, obligation: the act is commanded but is not part of the essence. Omit it and the Hajj is valid, and a dam is due: a sheep or a goat, or a seventh of a camel or a cow, slaughtered in the sanctuary and given to its poor.

Sunnah: the Prophet ﷺ did it or encouraged it. Omitting it costs nothing but loses something. Most of what makes the Hajj beautiful lives here.

One warning about the word dam. It literally means blood, and in these books it means a sacrifice, not a fine paid to anyone. It is meat for the poor of the sanctuary.

أحكام النِّساء

For women, and for the men travelling with them

ahkam an-nisa, the rulings concerning women

Most of the fear a woman carries into this journey is about one thing, and it has a clear answer given by the Prophet ﷺ to Aisha while she was crying. The rest of this section is dress, hair, travel, and the honest state of a question the schools have never agreed on.

Menstruation does not stop the Hajj

Sunnah

Aisha entered ihram for Umrah and began menstruating before reaching Makkah. The Prophet ﷺ came in and found her weeping. He said: this is a thing Allah has decreed upon the daughters of Adam. Do what the pilgrim does, except do not perform tawaf around the House until you become pure.

Read the exception carefully, because everything hangs on it. She does what the pilgrim does. She enters ihram, she says the talbiyah, she stands at Arafah, she stays at Muzdalifah, she stones the pillars, her sacrifice is made, she cuts her hair, she is in the Hajj entirely. One act is paused: tawaf. And with it, since sa’i follows a valid tawaf, the sa’i waits too.

So a woman who begins to bleed on the seventh of Dhul Hijjah loses nothing of the Hajj. She performs tawaf al-ifadah when she becomes pure, even if that is on the twelfth or later, and she is excused the farewell tawaf entirely.

She should still make ghusl at the miqat, as Asma bint Umays was told to do after giving birth at Dhul Hulayfah. The washing is for the state of ihram, not for purity from bleeding.

The hard case: the flight leaves and she is not pure

Scholars differ

Tawaf al-ifadah is a pillar. It cannot be skipped, it cannot be bought off, and until it is performed she is still in ihram, which means her husband remains unlawful to her. And modern travel puts a fixed departure date on a body that keeps its own calendar. This is the genuinely difficult problem of this section, and it deserves an honest answer rather than a comfortable one.

The classical majority is firm: purity is a condition of tawaf, so she waits, even if it costs her the flight. Groups have historically kept a woman behind with a mahram until she became pure.

A second position, argued by Ibn Taymiyyah and adopted by a number of contemporary scholars and fatwa councils for cases of real necessity, holds that a woman in this position may perform tawaf al-ifadah while menstruating, taking precautions, because the requirement of purity for tawaf is not established by an explicit text of the same strength as the obligation itself, and because the alternative places on her a hardship the law does not impose. Some who permit it require a sacrifice with it and some do not.

Do not decide this from a page. Decide it with a scholar who can hear the actual circumstances. But go knowing that the question has an answer and that scholars have thought about you.

Dress, hair, and prayer

Sunnah

There is no special ihram garment for a woman. She wears her ordinary modest clothing, in any colour she likes, and there is no requirement of white. What is prohibited to her specifically is the niqab, a veil cut and fitted to the face, and gloves, because those two were named. Her face and hands are otherwise uncovered in ihram, which is the point of naming them.

She may still lower a loose cloth from her head over her face when men are passing, which is what Aisha described the women doing on the journey. The schools differ over whether that cloth may rest against the face: the Hanafis hold it should be kept off it, by a stiffened edge or a cap, and others permit contact.

For the hair, taqsir only. She takes about a fingertip’s length from the ends, gathering the hair or taking from each braid. Women do not shave.

She may not pray or fast while menstruating, and she does not make up the prayers. She may make du’a, say dhikr, say the talbiyah, recite from memory according to many scholars, carry her supplication list, and stand at Arafah for the whole afternoon with her hands raised. None of that is closed to her, and Arafah is not a prayer.

Scholars differ

A mahram is a man a woman can never marry: her father, son, brother, uncle, grandfather, father in law, and her husband is counted with them. Editorial

Medication to delay a period is permitted by the great majority of contemporary scholars if a doctor says it is safe for her, and many women use it for exactly this journey. Start it well before travelling, not the week you fly, and expect it to be unreliable if you begin late.

When something goes wrong

al-jubran, the repairing of what was broken

It will. Buses fail, crowds turn people back, bodies give out, and somebody wears scented soap on the ninth. The law has a whole chapter for this, and its temper is merciful. Missing Arafah

Scholars differ

If dawn on the tenth arrives and you were not at Arafah at any point in the window, the Hajj is missed. The scholars call this fawat al-Hajj, and they set out what happens next rather than leaving a person stranded.

You come out of ihram by performing the acts of an Umrah: tawaf, sa’i, and cutting the hair. A sacrifice is due. And you make up the Hajj the following year. That is the position of the four schools, taken from the rulings of Umar and other companions on people this happened to.

It is worth knowing why the schools require the make up even when the Hajj was voluntary: the Qur’an commands the completion of the Hajj and the Umrah once entered, so entering voluntarily makes finishing binding.

Being prevented from finishing

Qur’an

If you are physically stopped from completing the rites, by a closed border, an illness that makes movement impossible, an arrest, an accident, the Qur’an addresses you directly: if you are prevented, then whatever sacrifice is easy. You slaughter where you are held, cut your hair, and come out of ihram.

There is a sunnah worth using before you ever leave home. Duba’ah bint az-Zubayr was ill and afraid she could not complete the journey. The Prophet ﷺ told her to perform Hajj and to stipulate a condition: O Allah, my place of release is wherever You hold me back. Whoever makes that condition at the moment of entering ihram, and is then genuinely prevented, comes out of ihram with no sacrifice due at all.

If you are elderly, unwell, or travelling with a condition that could stop you, say those words when you enter ihram. It costs nothing and it can save you a great deal. The Shafi’is and Hanbalis act on it fully; the Hanafis and Malikis do not give the condition the same effect.

The commonest version of this: you used the hotel’s scented soap, or a scented deodorant, or put on a shirt half asleep on a bus at four in the morning. The moment you realise, stop. Wash it off, take it off. Do not carry on because you have already ruined it, because you have not.

Whether an expiation is then due depends on the school, and this is one of the differences most worth carrying with you, because it decides whether you owe anything at all.

Under all four, a prohibition broken out of genuine need, an injury requiring a bandage, an illness requiring the head to be shaved, is permitted and carries the expiation without any sin, exactly as the Qur’an says of the one with an ailment of the head.

Where the schools part

Shafi’i and Hanbali

no expiation on the one who forgot or did not know, in perfume, clothing and covering the head, provided he stops at once.

Hanafi and Maliki

the standard summary is that the expiation is due even from the forgetful, because it repairs the rite rather than punishing the person, and the Hanafis reduce it to charity in a number of small cases. The summary flattens the Maliki position, which has recognised exceptions of its own, so ask before you assume you owe anything.

All four

hold that a prohibition done out of necessity is permitted and carries the expiation without sin.

Lost count, missed a throw, could not get there

Scholars differ

Lost count in tawaf or sa’i: build on the smaller number you are certain of, and complete. Uncertainty in worship is settled by certainty, the way it is in prayer.

Missed a whole day’s stoning: most scholars have you make it up on the following day, throwing the missed day’s pebbles first and then that day’s, in order. Missing all of it until the days are over costs a sacrifice. Missing one or two pebbles is treated more lightly and the schools differ on the amount of charity due.

Could not reach the pillars at all through illness, disability, pregnancy or crowd danger: appoint someone. He throws his own seven first, then yours, at each pillar. This is agreed and it is not a lesser Hajj.

Left Mina early, or missed a night: this is where the difference between the schools actually saves people, since the Hanafis do not attach a sacrifice to the nights at Mina at all. Ask your group’s scholar which view they act on before you pay for a sheep you may not owe.

The mistakes that cost nothing

Qur’an

Crying. Losing your temper and regretting it. Being unable to finish a du’a. Falling asleep at Arafah. Forgetting the words on Safa. Feeling nothing at all when you first see the Kaaba, while the person next to you weeps.

None of these are in any book of expiations, because none of them are defects. The Hajj is made of acts, and the acts were performed. What you felt while performing them is between you and Allah and is not always available on demand.

The sins of the tongue are different: they cost repentance, and where a person was hurt, they cost an apology. The Qur’an names them right at the start of the season for a reason.

Common mistakes, said gently

akhta shai’ah, common errors

None of these are written to embarrass anyone. Every one of them is done by sincere people who were never taught otherwise, and most of them come from loving the place too much rather than too little. Knowing them beforehand is the only cure, because nobody has time to explain in a crowd.

Fighting to reach the Black Stone

Sunnah

Kissing al-Hajar al-Aswad is a sunnah. Elbowing a seventy year old woman out of the way to do it is a sin. A sunnah does not sit on top of a wrong, and no scholar in any school has ever said otherwise.

Umar walked up to the Stone and said: I know that you are a stone, you neither harm nor benefit, and were it not that I saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ kissing you, I would not kiss you. It is reported that the Prophet ﷺ told Umar, who was strong, not to crowd at the Stone because he would harm the weak, and to face it and say the takbir if he could not reach it.

So: if there is space, kiss it. If not, raise your hand toward it, say Allahu akbar, and keep walking. You have done the sunnah. The version of this journey where you touch every stone and hurt three people is worth less than the version where you touch none.

Believing every circuit has its own du’a

Editorial

Books are sold at every gate with a different supplication printed for each circuit of tawaf, and for each stretch of the sa’i. Nothing of the sort is established from the Prophet ﷺ. The only fixed words inside tawaf are the takbir at the Stone and the verse said between the Yamani corner and the Black Stone.

Two things go wrong when a person reads from a card. He spends the tawaf watching the page instead of asking for anything he wants, and he is thrown into a panic when he loses his place. Say what is in you. In your own language. That is not a lesser tawaf, it is the tawaf.

There is no harm in someone reciting a printed du’a because it helps him. The mistake is believing it is required, and correcting others who do not have one.

Wiping the cloth, the walls, the Maqam

Editorial

The kiswah, the black cloth over the Kaaba, is replaced every year. Wiping it over your face, rubbing your children against it, tying threads to it or pressing your body against the walls and the corners for blessing has no basis in what the Prophet ﷺ or his companions did. Nor does touching the Maqam of Ibrahim, or the Yamani corner with the lips, or the other two corners at all.

Two things only were touched in the House: the Black Stone, kissed or touched, and the Yamani corner, touched with the hand. His companions were precise about this because they understood that in worship, adding is not loving more.

Standing at the multazam, the space between the Stone and the door, and making du’a there is reported of Ibn Abbas and some of the early generations, and a number of scholars permit it. It is also a place where people are routinely crushed. Know that it is a matter with some room in it, and that no one is required to reach it.

Thinking the pebbles hit devils

Scholars differ

People throw with rage. They shout. They throw shoes, umbrellas, water bottles, whole sandals, and every year someone throws a large stone that opens a stranger’s head, because they have been told they are stoning devils and they mean it.

There is no devil standing at the pillars. The pillars mark places, and the throwing is an act of obedience and remembrance. A report from Aisha states that the circling of the House, the going between Safa and Marwah, and the throwing of the pebbles were only established for the remembrance of Allah. Scholars have graded that report differently, and it is honest to say so, and its meaning is what every school teaches about these rites.

Throw seven small pebbles, the size of a chickpea, one at a time, saying Allahu akbar with each. The Prophet ﷺ held up such pebbles and said: with the like of these, throw. And in the same breath he warned against exaggeration in religion, and said that exaggeration is what destroyed the peoples before us. He said that at the Jamarat, on that morning, holding stones the size of beans.

Arriving at Arafah with nothing left

Editorial

The commonest way to waste a Hajj is to spend everything before the ninth. Repeated Umrahs from at-Tan’im every other day. Night prayer until Fajr for a week. Shopping until midnight. Walking twenty kilometres a day in the sun in early Dhul Hijjah. Then Arafah arrives and the person sleeps through the afternoon of his life, or sits with a migraine unable to form a sentence.

Treat the days before as training and rest, not as the event. Drink water constantly, sleep in the afternoons, take your prayers in your room when the mosque is a forty minute walk in forty five degree heat, and let the extra Umrahs go. There is no established sunnah of repeating Umrah again and again in one journey; Aisha’s Umrah from at-Tan’im was a consolation for one that her period had interrupted.

Arrive at Arafah with something left. That is the whole strategy of the Hajj.

Guarding the rites and losing the people

Sunnah

A man can be meticulous about ramal in the first three circuits and shove a stranger out of a queue for water an hour later. Everyone has seen it. Some of us have been it.

The Hajj is a rare place where every single interaction is with someone tired, hot, frightened, far from home and speaking another language. Feeding people, giving up a place, carrying a bag, being gentle in speech: when the Prophet ﷺ was asked what makes a Hajj accepted, the answer reported from him is feeding others and speaking well.

And know the boundary of what the journey erases. The Hajj wipes what is between a person and his Lord. It does not wipe what is owed to people: money you took, a reputation you damaged, a family member you cut off. The bankrupt of this ummah, he ﷺ said, is the one who comes with prayers and fasting and charity, and also with having insulted this one and struck that one, and his good deeds are handed over until they run out.

Settle it before you go, if you can. And if you cannot, go, and settle it when you return.

Provision, and the best of it

Qur’an

The Qur’an gives the packing instruction in the middle of the Hajj verses, and the whole sentence is worth memorising: take provision, and indeed the best provision is taqwa, being mindful of Allah. Commentators say it was revealed about people who set out for Hajj with no food, calling themselves reliant on Allah, and then begged from the pilgrims.

So the two halves belong together. Pack properly, because arriving as a burden on other people is not piety. And know that the sandals and the sachets are not what you are actually carrying there.

Two pieces of white cloth

Editorial

A man in ihram is dressed in two unstitched white sheets. He will be dressed in something very similar once more, and he will not be putting it on himself.

That is not a morbid thought, it is the design. In the two cloths there is no way to tell a surgeon from a driver, a citizen of one country from a refugee of another, a man with three properties from a man with three shirts. Every marker a person spends his life assembling is taken off at a line in the desert. What is left standing on the plain at Arafah is the thing that will be raised, which is all any of us ever were.

People say the Hajj is a rehearsal for the Day of Standing. It is more exact than that. It is the same posture, the same clothing, the same crowd, the same helplessness, the same asking. The difference is that this time you chose to come, and you can still ask.

Returning as the day his mother bore him

Sunnah

Whoever performs Hajj for Allah and does not speak obscenely nor act corruptly returns as the day his mother bore him. Sit with that sentence. Not forgiven a little. Not improved. Returned to the state of a newborn, who has never done anything at all.

A newborn has no history. That is the gift, and it is also the difficulty: a person returns with a clean page and immediately has to decide what to write on it. The scholars said the sign of an accepted Hajj is not what you felt at Arafah but what changed afterwards, and they said it because they had watched people come back unchanged.

So the last question of this room is a plain one. If Allah has just given you a page with nothing on it, what is the first thing you intend to put there.

And then the ordinary life

Qur’an

The Qur’an ends the Hajj passage by telling the pilgrims to remember Allah the way they used to remember their forefathers, or more intensely, and then it divides people by what they ask for: some say our Lord, give us in this world, and they have no share in the hereafter; and others say our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire.

Then, immediately, the very next verses turn to ordinary life: the days of Tashriq, the person whose speech about worldly life impresses you while his heart is at war, the one who sells himself seeking the pleasure of Allah. The Book moves from the plain of Arafah back to the street without pausing, because that was always where this was going.

You will land at an airport. Someone will be late to collect you. Within a week there will be a queue, a bill, a rude message. That week is the Hajj too. It is where the standing gets spent, or does not.
