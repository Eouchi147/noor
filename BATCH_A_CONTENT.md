## BADR — The Day of Furqan
**17 Ramadan 2H / March 624 CE**

### Summary
The first major battle. A small band of believers faced a Quraysh army roughly three times their size. Allah named it the Day of Furqan — the criterion that distinguished truth from falsehood.

### Facts
- Date: 17 Ramadan 2H (13 or 17 March 624 CE in most reckonings)
- Muslim force: approximately 313 men, 70 camels, 2 horses
- Quraysh force: approximately 950–1000 men, 100 horses, 700 camels
- Location: the wells of Badr, southwest of Madinah
- Muslim martyrs: 14
- Quraysh dead: approximately 70 (including key leaders)
- Captives: approximately 70
- Primary Qur’an: 3:123–125, 8:9–12, 8:17, 8:42–44

### Narrative

After the Hijrah the economic pressure on Quraysh intensified. The Muslims began intercepting caravans that had once moved freely between Makkah and Syria. When news reached Madinah that a major caravan under Abu Sufyan was returning from Syria, the Prophet ﷺ set out with a small force intending only to intercept the goods, not to seek a pitched battle.

Abu Sufyan learned of the movement, changed route, and sent a rider to Makkah. Quraysh rose in force. The decision was no longer about a caravan; it became a matter of honour and the destruction of the new community in Madinah. Nearly a thousand men marched north.

The Muslim force that reached the wells of Badr numbered around three hundred and thirteen. They had seventy camels (ridden in turns) and only two horses. Many were young. Some were still recovering from the hardships of the Hijrah and the earlier boycott. The Prophet ﷺ arranged them at the wells, denying Quraysh easy water. The night before the battle a light rain fell, firming the sand under Muslim feet and making the ground heavier for the Quraysh advance.

Before the fighting the Prophet ﷺ raised his hands in prolonged dua. He asked Allah for the victory He had promised. He continued until his cloak fell from his shoulders. Abu Bakr came behind him, picked up the cloak, and said words to the effect: “Enough, O Messenger of Allah. Your Lord will fulfil His promise to you.” The ranks were set. Champions came forward for single combat according to Arab custom. Hamza, Ali, and Ubaydah ibn al-Harith met the Quraysh champions; two were killed, one mortally wounded.

Then the general engagement began. The Qur’an records that the believers sought help and were answered with a thousand angels, ranks upon ranks. The Prophet ﷺ took a handful of dust and threw it toward the enemy; the Qur’an later said “You did not throw when you threw, but Allah threw.” The leaders of Quraysh fell. Abu Jahl was killed. The field turned. Approximately seventy of Quraysh were killed and seventy taken captive. Fourteen Muslims were martyred.

The day was named Yawm al-Furqan — the Day of Distinction. For the first time the two parties stood clearly opposed in open battle and the outcome was decisive. The treatment of the captives became an early test of the community’s character; ransom was accepted for many, and some who could teach literacy were set free in exchange for teaching the children of Madinah.

Classical sources (Ibn Ishaq, Ibn Hisham, and later historians) preserve the lists of participants, the names of the dead, and the reports of the angels. The theological weight is consistent across the tradition: victory came from Allah, not from numbers or equipment. The few who stood with the Messenger were made firm, and the many who came to extinguish the light were turned back.

### Qur’an
3:123 — “And already had Allah given you victory at Badr while you were few in number. Then fear Allah that you may be grateful.”
8:9 — “When you asked help of your Lord, and He answered you, ‘Indeed, I will reinforce you with a thousand from the angels, following one another.’”
8:17 — “You did not kill them, but Allah killed them. And you did not throw when you threw, but Allah threw.”

### Hadith (selection)
- The prolonged dua of the Prophet until his cloak fell, and Abu Bakr’s words (reported in multiple collections).
- Reports concerning the participation of the angels and the naming of the day as Furqan.
- Lists of the fourteen martyrs preserved in the early sira.

### Impact / Ibrah
Badr established that numerical inferiority is not decisive when Allah’s help is present. It publicly distinguished the party of the Messenger from the party of Quraysh. It created the first major set of martyrs whose names entered the permanent memory of the ummah. It also set the ethical tone for the treatment of captives. From this day the political and military reality of the Hijrah community changed; Quraysh could no longer treat the Muslims as a minor irritant.

## UHUD — The Trial of Obedience
**Shawwal 3H / March 625 CE**

### Summary
The archers left their post. The Prophet was wounded. Seventy were martyred, including Hamza. Uhud became the school of discipline and the reminder that partial obedience is disobedience.

### Facts
- Date: 15 Shawwal 3H (approximately)
- Muslim force: ~700 (after hypocrites withdrew)
- Quraysh force: ~3000
- Archers ordered to hold the hill: 50
- Muslim martyrs: ~70 (including Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib)
- Prophet wounded: face, tooth broken
- Key Qur’an: 3:121–180 (especially 3:152–155)

### Narrative (core arcs)
Quraysh returned seeking revenge for Badr. The Prophet ﷺ positioned fifty archers on the hill with explicit orders not to leave their post regardless of the course of the battle. Early in the fighting the Muslims gained the upper hand. Seeing the enemy appear to break, many of the archers left their position to collect spoils. Khalid ibn al-Walid (still then with Quraysh) exploited the gap, led a cavalry charge around the hill, and struck the Muslim flank and rear.

Confusion spread. The Prophet ﷺ was surrounded, wounded in the face, and a tooth was broken. Rumour briefly spread that he had been killed. Seventy Muslims were martyred. Hamza, the Lion of Allah, was killed and his body later mutilated by Hind bint Utbah. The Qur’an addressed the community with both consolation and correction: the setback was a test, a consequence of disobedience, and a purification.

Uhud did not reverse the strategic direction set by Badr, but it taught a permanent lesson: the orders of the Messenger are not conditional on the apparent success of the moment. The archers’ hill remains the emblem of that lesson.

### Impact
Uhud is the school of obedience. It showed that victory is not automatic even for the companions of the Prophet. It produced the master of martyrs, Hamza. It also prepared the community for the longer trials of the later years, including the Trench.

## KHANDAQ — The Trench
**Shawwal 5H / 627 CE**

### Summary
A coalition of ten thousand. Salman al-Farisi suggested a trench. For nearly a month the Confederates were held. Allah sent a wind. The coalition broke. After Khandaq the initiative shifted permanently to the Muslims.

### Facts
- Date: Shawwal 5H
- Muslim force: ~3000
- Confederate force: ~10,000 (Quraysh, Ghatafan, and others)
- Trench: approximately 5.5 km long, dug in days under hunger and cold
- Key innovation: Persian-style defensive trench (Salman’s counsel)
- Key Qur’an: Surah al-Ahzab 33:9–27
- Outcome: Coalition withdrew without decisive battle; Banu Qurayza episode followed

### Narrative (core arcs)
The Banu Nadir, expelled earlier, worked to assemble a grand coalition. Quraysh, Ghatafan, and other tribes gathered the largest army Arabia had seen directed against Madinah. The Prophet ﷺ consulted. Salman al-Farisi said that in Persia, when cavalry was feared, a trench was dug. The idea was adopted. The Muslims dug for days in hard ground, in cold and hunger; the Prophet himself worked. A trench sealed the northern approach.

The Confederates arrived and could not cross in force. A long siege followed. Hypocrites wavered. Banu Qurayza’s neutrality collapsed into open threat. The distress reached its peak. Then Allah sent a bitter wind that overturned tents, extinguished fires, and filled eyes with dust. The coalition fractured and withdrew.

The Qur’an records the favour: “We sent against them a wind and forces you did not see.” After Khandaq the Prophet ﷺ indicated that the strategic posture had changed: the Muslims would now take the initiative.

### Impact
Khandaq proved that sound counsel can come from outside the Arab tribal repertoire. It demonstrated the value of defensive depth and patience. It shattered the possibility of a united Arabian front against Madinah. From this point the road toward Hudaybiyyah and the Opening of Makkah opened.

## HUDAYBIYYAH — The Manifest Victory
**Dhu al-Qi’dah 6H / 628 CE**

### Summary
A treaty that looked like a defeat. Bay’at al-Ridwan under the tree. Surah al-Fath revealed: “Indeed We have given you a clear victory.” Within two years the fruits were visible; within two years Makkah opened.

### Facts
- Date: Dhu al-Qi’dah 6H
- Muslim force: ~1400 (in ihram, essentially unarmed for battle)
- Location: Hudaybiyyah, ~22 km from Makkah
- Pledge: Bay’at al-Ridwan under the Samura tree
- Treaty terms: 10-year truce; return this year; Umrah the following year; other clauses that appeared one-sided
- Key Qur’an: Surah al-Fath 48:1–29 (especially 48:1, 48:18)
- Outcome: Strategic victory disguised as concession; rapid subsequent growth of Islam

### Narrative (core arcs)
The Prophet ﷺ saw a dream of entering the Sacred Mosque. He set out for Umrah with approximately fourteen hundred companions. Quraysh blocked them at Hudaybiyyah. Negotiations produced a treaty whose terms appeared humiliating to many companions: the Muslims would turn back that year, perform Umrah only the next year, and accept other conditions that seemed to favour Quraysh. Umar voiced the pain. Abu Bakr affirmed that Muhammad was the Messenger of Allah and would not be abandoned.

Under a tree the companions pledged (Bay’at al-Ridwan). Allah declared His pleasure with those who pledged under the tree. On the return journey Surah al-Fath was revealed, naming the episode a clear victory. The years that followed confirmed the verdict: the truce allowed free movement of ideas; major figures entered Islam; the political isolation of Quraysh increased; and the Opening of Makkah came in 8H with ten thousand, largely without bloodshed.

### Impact
Hudaybiyyah is the masterclass in strategic patience. What looked like concession was the opening of a greater door. The Qur’an itself named it victory while many still felt the sting of the terms. It remains the standing proof that divine assessment of events can differ from immediate human perception.
