How the River Branched

One Book, one qibla, one Prophet ﷺ, so why are there madhhabs, Sunni and Shia, Sufis and Salafis? A calm map of how the schools formed, what they actually disagree about, and how much of the river never branched at all.

١ Start with the trunk, not the branches

Every conversation about Islam's divisions should begin with an embarrassing fact for the dividers: the overwhelming mass of the religion is held in common by everyone who prays toward Makkah. Before a single difference is named, a Muslim of any school believes in Allah alone, His angels, His Books, His messengers, the Last Day and the decree; prays five times toward one House; fasts one month; gives one alms; recites one Qur'an, letter for letter, from Jakarta to Casablanca.

٢ The river in time

610-632 CE

The spring

Revelation. The Prophet ﷺ is the living answer to every question; there are no schools because there is no need.

632-661

The Rightly Guided Caliphs

Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali. The Qur'an is compiled into one mushaf. At the era's end, political crisis, Uthman's murder, then civil war, plants the seeds of the first groupings: those who would become Shia ("the party of Ali"), the Kharijites who left both sides, and the main body.

680

Karbala

Husayn ibn Ali, the Prophet's ﷺ grandson, is killed with his family. Grief over Karbala becomes the emotional heart of Shia identity forever after, and a sorrow shared by every Muslim.

700s-800s

The schools crystallize

As Islam spreads across empires, jurists systematize how to derive law: Abu Hanifa (d. 767), Malik (d. 795), ash-Shafi'i (d. 820), Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d. 855). Hadith is sifted, Bukhari (d. 870) and Muslim (d. 875) compile their Sahihs. In parallel, creed is defended in debate: al-Ash'ari (d. 936) and al-Maturidi (d. 944).

900s-1800s

The long settlement

The four Sunni law schools and Twelver Ja'fari law stabilize and coexist for a millennium. Sufi orders organize the science of the heart. Scholarship flows between all of them.

1800s-today

The modern currents

Colonialism, print, then the internet break old structures. Reform movements rise: some calling back to the earliest generations (Salafi), some defending the classical schools (traditionalist), some rereading law for new realities (modernist). Most Muslims today still simply pray, fast, and inherit their region's school without ever naming it.

٣ The four schools of law, why honest scholars differ

The madhhabs are not sects. They are four rigorous methods of answering the same question, what would the Prophet ﷺ have me do?, built by men who revered each other. They differ because the evidence itself allows range: two authentic hadith describe two ways he ﷺ did a thing; a word carries two readings; a practice of Madinah differs from a narration of Kufa. Disagreement here is not corruption of the religion. It is the shadow cast by richness.

Hanafi

Abu Hanifa · d. 767, Kufa

The jurist's jurist: systematic reasoning, hypothetical cases, mercy in commerce. Historically the school of empires, Ottomans, Mughals. Most followed madhhab today: South Asia, Turkey, Central Asia, the Balkans.

Maliki

Malik ibn Anas · d. 795, Madinah

Anchored in the living practice of Madinah, the city that watched revelation happen. Author of the Muwatta. Dominant across North and West Africa.

Shafi'i

Ash-Shafi'i · d. 820, Egypt

Student of Malik, teacher of Ahmad. Wrote ar-Risalah, the first book of legal theory, the grammar all schools now speak. Followed in Egypt, East Africa, Yemen, Southeast Asia.

Hanbali

Ahmad ibn Hanbal · d. 855, Baghdad

The hadith master who suffered the inquisition rather than bend the creed. Textual, cautious, principled. Today the reference school of Saudi Arabia and Qatar.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "When a judge strives to reach a ruling and is correct, he has two rewards; and when he strives and errs, he has one reward."

Bukhari 7352 · Muslim 1716, the charter of honest disagreement
And the popular saying?

"The differing of my ummah is a mercy" is quoted everywhere, but it has no authentic chain to the Prophet ﷺ; hadith specialists grade it baseless as a Prophetic saying. The idea survives on better evidence: the verse of ijtihad-reward above, and the practice of the companions, who differed and still prayed behind one another. This Codex tells you when a beloved quote is weak, that honesty is itself a school.

Shia Islam has its own school of law, Ja'fari fiqh, from Ja'far as-Sadiq (d. 765), a scholar the Sunni founders also honored: Abu Hanifa and Malik both narrated from him.

٤ The creed schools, one God, three grammars

A different set of schools formed around a different question: how do we speak about Allah's attributes, His hand, His throne, His descending, without either imagining Him like a creature or emptying the words of meaning?

Athari

Affirm the texts as they came, without asking how, without comparison. "The istiwa is known; the how is unknown", Malik's famous answer. The posture of the earliest generations and the Hanbali school.

Ash'ari

Defend the same God with the tools of reason, arguing against philosophers on their own field, interpreting some attributes to guard transcendence. The framework of most classical Shafi'i and Maliki scholarship.

Maturidi

Al-Ash'ari's contemporary in Samarqand, nearly identical conclusions with its own method; historically paired with the Hanafi school across the East.

For a thousand years, these three sat together in the same mosques and often the same scholar. The overwhelming instinct of mainstream scholarship: all three are attempts to protect the same sentence, "There is nothing like Him" (Ash-Shura 42:11).

٥ Sunni and Shia, the honest map

The split was not born in a debate about God. It was born in a question of leadership: after the Prophet ﷺ, should authority pass by the community's choice (the view that became Sunni, ~85-90% of Muslims today) or did it belong by designation to Ali and the Prophet's house (the view that became Shia, ~10-13%)? Politics hardened into theology over generations, above all after Karbala.

What Twelver Shia distinctly hold: a line of twelve divinely-appointed Imams from Ali's house, the last in occultation; Ja'fari law; mourning rites for Karbala. What both hold: everything in the trunk above, one Qur'an, one prayer's direction, one Ramadan, love of the Prophet's family (which Sunnis also count as faith: the Prophet ﷺ said of Hasan and Husayn, "they are my two sweet basils in this world", Bukhari 5994).

How this Codex speaks of it

This site is written from the broad Sunni mainstream, and it refuses the internet's favorite sport. The scholars' rule across centuries: whoever prays our prayer, faces our qibla and eats our slaughter is a Muslim, with a Muslim's rights (Bukhari 391). Disagreement is documented in these pages; contempt is not.

٦ Sufism, the science of the heart, and its counterfeits

Tasawwuf began as a job description, not a sect: the discipline of purifying the heart, sincerity, remembrance, breaking the ego, drawn from the Prophet's ﷺ own nights. In its sober classical form it lived inside the law, not around it: al-Ghazali the Shafi'i jurist, an-Nawawi the hadith master, and countless madhhab scholars were shaped by it. Its orders carried Islam to West Africa and Southeast Asia.

And like every science, it grew counterfeits: shrine-cults, claims of shaykhs above the shariah, innovations dressed as devotion, practices the sober Sufis themselves condemned first. The honest summary: the purification of the soul is obligatory (Qur'an: "successful is the one who purifies it," Ash-Shams 91:9); the excesses are not the science, they are its disease.

٧ Today's landscape in one honest paragraph each

Traditionalists

Follow one of the four schools with its classical creed, as most of the ummah always has. Strength: continuity and depth. Risk they name themselves: rigidity where the world has genuinely moved.

Salafis

Call back to the understanding of the first three generations, prioritizing text over school loyalty. Strength: scriptural seriousness, cleansing of superstition. Risk they name themselves: harshness, and a tendency to make method into membership.

Modernists

Rereading the tradition for contemporary questions, finance, citizenship, science. Strength: engaging the actual world. Risk: reading the world into the text instead of the text into the world.

The Prophet ﷺ said the ummah would divide into many paths, the famous "seventy-three" narration, with safety for "what I and my companions are upon." Scholars caution: the number is style for "many," and the hadith is a warning against division, not a hunting license. No group's name appears in it.

Abu Dawud 4596 · Tirmidhi 2641 · hasan; wording varies

٨ Men and women in the law

Spiritually, the Qur'an flattens every hierarchy: it lists believing men and believing women side by side ten times in a single verse and promises both "forgiveness and an immense reward" (Al-Ahzab 33:35). The most knowledgeable transmitter of the Prophet's ﷺ private sunnah was a woman, Aisha, whom senior companions consulted. The world's oldest continuously operating university, al-Qarawiyyin in Fes, was founded by a woman, Fatima al-Fihri, in 859.

Legally, the schools assign some different rulings, dress, prayer exemptions during menstruation, the financial asymmetry in which mahr and family maintenance are owed by men, differences the jurists ground in role and responsibility, not in rank of soul. Where culture has smuggled in oppression and labeled it religion, forced marriage, denial of education, honour violence, the law itself testifies against the smugglers: the Prophet ﷺ annulled the marriage of a woman wed without consent (Bukhari 5138), and made the pursuit of knowledge an obligation on every Muslim (Ibn Majah 224 · sahih li-ghayrih).

إِنَّ الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَالْمُسْلِمَاتِ وَالْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتِ ... أَعَدَّ اللَّهُ لَهُم مَّغْفِرَةً وَأَجْرًا عَظِيمًا

"The Muslim men and Muslim women, the believing men and believing women…, Allah has prepared for them forgiveness and an immense reward."

Qur'an · Al-Ahzab 33:35

"Hold fast to the rope of Allah, all together, and do not divide." (Aal Imran 3:103). The river branched; the water is one. Learn the branches so that no one can frighten you with them, then go back to drinking.
