The Ninety-Nine Names

Every other room in this library has something you can draw. This one has the names He gave Himself, and the light they are read by. The Book spends as much of itself saying what He is not as saying what He is, and that is where this room begins.

Surah al-Ikhlas · 112

Four lines, and the question is answered

The Prophet ﷺ was asked to describe his Lord. The answer that came down is four short verses, and it is mostly a list of what He is not. He is not begotten. He does not beget. Nothing is comparable to Him. The shortest complete theology in the Book spends three quarters of itself clearing the ground.

Al-Asma al-Husna · The Most Beautiful Names

Ninety-nine, and He is one

Touch any name to sit with it. Each one is a door, not a definition: the Arabic carries a weight no English line can hold, and the line beneath is a hand pointing, not the thing itself.

99 names

    This is the enumeration narrated in Jami' at-Tirmidhi. The hadith that the names are ninety-nine is agreed upon; the list itself is a narration, and scholars have differed over which ninety-nine it names. The Arabic is the name. The English beneath it is a translation, and every translation of a name of God is a smaller thing than the name.

    What He is not

    The Book clears the ground before it builds

    Most of what a person gets wrong about God is a picture they brought with them. The Qur'an spends a great deal of itself taking pictures away, and every one of these is a sentence of denial rather than description.

    Al-Hashr 59:22-24

    Fourteen of them arrive in one breath

    The names are not scattered evenly through the Book. At the end of Surah al-Hashr fourteen of them come one after another in three verses, which is the densest the Qur'an ever gets about who He is. Press the button and watch where they fall in the ninety-nine.

    Ayat al-Kursi · 2:255

    Neither drowsiness nor sleep

    لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ

    Every created thing that keeps watch eventually stops keeping watch. The verse the Prophet ﷺ called the greatest in the Book makes its case by naming a limit and then denying it: the One holding the heavens and the earth in place is not tired by holding them.

    وَلَا يَئُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَا

    And their preservation does not weary Him

    Why they were given

    They were not given to be listed

    A name is for calling with. The command in the Qur'an is not to memorise the ninety-nine but to use them, and the classical practice is to match the name to the need: to ask the Provider for provision, the Forgiving for forgiveness, the Guide when you do not know which way to go.

    وَلِلَّهِ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ فَادْعُوهُ بِهَا

    To Allah belong the most beautiful names, so call upon Him by them · Qur'an 7:180

    Tawhid · The three doors

    One, in three ways of saying it

    The classical scholars divided the oneness of God into three, not because He is three, but because there are three ways a person forgets it.

    الرُّبُوبِيَّة

    Lordship

    That He alone creates, owns and sustains. The part almost everyone concedes, and the part that settles nothing on its own.
