The Last Sermon

On the ninth of Dhul-Hijjah in the year 10 AH, the Prophet ﷺ rode into the valley at Arafah and addressed the largest gathering of his life, more than a hundred thousand pilgrims who had come to learn their Hajj from him. He was sixty-three, about three months from his death, and his words show he sensed it. That same day, while he stood at Arafah, the verse descended: ’This day I have perfected for you your religion, and completed My favor upon you, and approved for you Islam as religion.’ (5:3) What follows is his testament, spoken while the religion itself was being sealed.

Read this first. The text most widely shared online as ’The Last Sermon’ is a composite: several narrations of different dates and different strength, stitched into one smooth speech. Honesty requires saying so plainly. The core is beyond doubt: Sahih Muslim 1218, the long hadith of Jabir, preserves the Arafah khutbah itself, with the sanctity of blood and wealth, the abolition of the blood claims and riba of Jahiliyyah beginning with the Prophet’s ﷺ own family, the rights of and duties toward women, and holding fast to the Book of Allah. Other beloved lines are authentic but were spoken at Mina during the same pilgrimage, above all ’there is no superiority of Arab over non-Arab except by taqwa,’ which comes from a separate authentic narration in Musnad Ahmad, not from the Arafah text. A few flourishes in circulating versions have no strong chain at all. Every section below carries a label, so that what you read here stands exactly as the evidence stands.

I May Not Meet You After This Year Sunnah-confirmed

Jabir ibn Abdullah, who preserved the fullest account of that pilgrimage, describes the Prophet ﷺ riding his camel al-Qaswa into the belly of the valley and calling the people to hear him. Through those days he kept saying: ’Take your rites from me, for I do not know; perhaps I shall not perform Hajj after this Hajj of mine.’ The companions heard the farewell inside the instruction, and the journey itself came to be named Hajjat al-Wada, the Farewell Pilgrimage. So everything said at Arafah carries the weight of a man setting his house in order before the largest audience he would ever have, saying, while there was still time, what must never be forgotten.

Muslim 1218 · Muslim 1297

The Sanctity of Blood, Wealth and Honor Sunnah-confirmed

He began where every narration of that day begins: ’Indeed your blood and your wealth are sacred to you, as sacred as this day of yours, in this month of yours, in this land of yours.’ His listeners measured holiness by three things above all: the day of Arafah, the month of Dhul-Hijjah, the precincts of the Haram. He took the most inviolable things they knew and made every believer’s life and property their equal. In the khutbah at Mina days later he ﷺ joined a third term to the pair: ’your blood, your wealth, and your honor.’ Reputation, dignity, the good name of another Muslim, all fenced by the same sanctity. Whoever carries this sermon leaves it knowing that the person beside him is sacred ground.

Muslim 1218 · Bukhari 1741

Jahiliyyah Placed Beneath His Feet Sunnah-confirmed

Then he dismantled the old world: ’Every matter of Jahiliyyah is laid down beneath these two feet of mine.’ The blood feuds of the age of ignorance were cancelled, and he began with his own house: ’The first blood claim I abolish is our own blood, the blood of the son of Rabi’ah ibn al-Harith,’ a child of his kinsman, killed as a nursling among Banu Sa’d. The riba of Jahiliyyah, its usury, was annulled the same way: ’The first riba I abolish is our riba, the riba of Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib; it is cancelled, all of it.’ He demanded of his own family first what he demanded of everyone, and no reform in history has begun closer to home.

Muslim 1218

The Rights of Women Sunnah-confirmed

’Fear Allah concerning women, for you have taken them by the covenant of Allah, and their companionship has been made lawful to you by the word of Allah.’ In his final public teaching, marriage is a trust sworn in the name of Allah. The duties run both ways: a wife is not to admit into the home anyone her husband dislikes, and ’their right upon you is their provision and their clothing, according to what is honorable.’ The clause permitting, after betrayal of that trust, a discipline ’without severity’ was fenced tightly by the jurists and never once practiced by the Prophet ﷺ, who struck no woman and no servant, ever. Tirmidhi’s farewell narration adds his ﷺ plain command: ’Treat women well.’

Muslim 1218 · Muslim 2328 · Tirmidhi 1163

One Body of Brothers Sunnah-confirmed

The sanctities knit the believers into a single family, and the gravest breach of them is to draw a brother’s blood. During that same pilgrimage he ﷺ told Jarir to call the people to silence, then said: ’Do not return after me to being unbelievers, striking the necks of one another.’ To kill a believer is to fall back into the darkness that had just been placed under his feet. Property follows the same rule: he taught that nothing of a Muslim’s wealth is lawful to another except what is given with a willing soul. And his ﷺ standing definition held the rest together: ’The Muslim is the brother of the Muslim: he does not wrong him, does not forsake him, does not despise him.’

Bukhari 121 · Bukhari 1741 · Muslim 2564 · Musnad Ahmad

No Superiority Except by Taqwa Debated

The most quoted line of the famous composite does not come from the Arafah text preserved in Muslim; it comes from a khutbah during the days of Mina, on the same pilgrimage, a few days later: ’O people, your Lord is one, and your father is one. No Arab has superiority over a non-Arab, nor a non-Arab over an Arab; no red over black, nor black over red, except by taqwa.’ The words are authentically his ﷺ: the narration in Musnad Ahmad carries a chain graded sahih by the hadith editors of our era. It is marked debated here for placement only, because honesty requires saying that this declaration belongs to Mina, not to the Arafah sermon into which the composite stitches it.

Musnad Ahmad 23489

What He Left Behind: The Book of Allah Sunnah-confirmed

’I have left among you that which, if you hold fast to it, you will never go astray: the Book of Allah.’ This is the wording preserved in Muslim’s account of Arafah, and it is the sermon’s center of gravity: the community was about to lose its Prophet ﷺ, never its guidance. Two related wordings deserve honest labels. ’The Book of Allah and my Sunnah’ appears in later collections through chains the scholars grade variously; it is not in the sahih Arafah text. And the ’two weighty things,’ the Book of Allah and the Prophet’s ﷺ household, is authentic in Muslim but belongs to Ghadir Khumm, on the road home after the Hajj. At Arafah itself, the anchor he named was the Book.

Muslim 1218 · Muslim 2408

Let the Present Convey to the Absent Sunnah-confirmed

The sermon was not meant for the valley alone. In the khutbah at Mina he ﷺ commanded: ’Let the one who is present convey it to the one who is absent, for perhaps the one present will convey it to someone who retains it better than he.’ One sentence turned a hundred thousand listeners into transmitters, and everything that followed, the memorizers, the written collections, the sciences built to test every link of every chain, unfolds from it. It also quietly includes you. Fourteen centuries of conveying were set in motion so that these words would find the absent, and the absent, that day, meant every Muslim not yet born.

Bukhari 1741 · Bukhari 67

O Allah, Bear Witness Sunnah-confirmed

He closed by handing the verdict on his own mission first to his people, then to his Lord. ’You will be asked about me. What will you say?’ They answered: ’We bear witness that you have conveyed, fulfilled, and given sincere counsel.’ Then he raised his forefinger toward the sky and turned it down toward the people, saying: ’O Allah, bear witness. O Allah, bear witness,’ three times. A hundred thousand witnesses, and the Witness above them all. Then Bilal called the adhan, and the Prophet ﷺ prayed Zuhr and Asr joined together in the valley, and stood in supplication at Arafah until the sun set. About three months later he returned to his Lord, and the trust he confirmed that day remains conveyed.
